## Why this file exists and what it does not do

This is a **Path A recovery** (recover as historical draft, not silently treat as canon) rather than
a **Path B lineage-only record** (retain findings, not wording), because the operator supplied the
full text and its provenance is traceable to a single paste with no intervening edit by this agent.
The distinction matters because a v0.2 stopping rule specifically was cited as load-bearing by later
work (v0.5 amendment §A6.1) without the source text being available to verify the citation against —
exactly the failure mode a recovered-but-unratified draft is supposed to close.

**Recovering this text does not resolve v0.5 amendment §A6.1.** Resolving that item requires an
operator decision to either (a) formally bind v0.5's stopping rule to this recovered §38 by an
explicit ratification act, recorded the way any other protocol provision is ratified, or (b) author
a new stopping rule for v0.5 now, superseding this one. Until one of those two acts happens, §38
below is historical evidence only — readable, citable as lineage, not governing.

IRL Structural Invariant Research Programme
EXP-P2 — Invariant-Governed Consequence Engineering
Version: 0.2
Status: Revised pre-registration design for second adversarial review
Date: 22 July 2026
Research class: Structural invariant science
Primary scientific focus: Consequence-bound generation and verification
Relationship to EXP-P1: Complementary and independently interpretable
Review requested: Red-team, blue-team, scientific-integrity, and constitutional review

### 0. Revision basis

This version incorporates the findings of an independent blind critical review of EXP-P2 v0.1.
The review accepted P2 as scientifically distinct from P1 and recommended proceeding after material
revisions.

The principal revisions are:

1. the primary claim has been recentered on an invariant-governed generation and verification
   pipeline, rather than invariant wording alone;
2. total expert effort to a defined acceptance threshold replaces raw repair count as the primary
   outcome;
3. a generic second-pass review arm has been added to control for additional inference and compute;
4. the prose and invariant conditions will be semantically content-matched and independently
   audited;
5. representation-at-generation has been retained as a narrower secondary hypothesis;
6. the task substrate has been expanded across multiple object classes and information-sufficiency
   levels;
7. invariant authorship, validation, evaluation, and adjudication are separated;
8. in-set and out-of-set failure modes will be reported separately;
9. evaluator blinding and output normalization are mandatory;
10. physical fabrication will anchor the expert-review and simulation pipeline;
11. "pre-paid reasoning" has been removed as an inference-compute claim;
12. a programme-level stopping rule has been added;
13. the distinction between the invariant object and its textual projection has been made explicit.

This document remains a review draft. No experimental materials are frozen until the second
adversarial review is complete.

### 1. Purpose

EXP-P2 tests whether an invariant-governed artificial-intelligence pipeline can reduce the work and
failure involved in converting a generated design into a physically valid artifact.

It moves the principal outcome beyond linguistic quality.

The experiment does not ask only:

Did the model produce a convincing answer?

It asks:

What happened when that answer was required to satisfy independently testable conditions and
survive contact with physical reality?

P2 therefore treats inference as one stage in a larger process:

Intent → condition projection → generation → verification → repair → realization → observed
consequence

The experiment is intended to test a bounded structural claim. It is not intended to validate the
complete invariant-intelligence thesis.

### 2. Programme context

The Invariant Research Lab investigates whether validated invariants can improve the reliability,
efficiency, and governability of intelligent systems.

An invariant is a condition that must remain true for a system, object, process, or outcome to
remain valid within a defined scope.

The programme currently distinguishes three functions:

2.1 Compression

Previously completed reasoning can be preserved in a reusable representation.

2.2 Orchestration

The conditions relevant to an intent can be selected and projected into a task.

2.3 Governance

Generated artifacts can be checked against governing conditions before consequential action is
permitted.

P1 primarily investigates compression and reasoning preservation.

P2 primarily investigates consequence-bound orchestration and governance.

A later experiment may investigate invariant representation as a mathematical or topological object.
Any such discovery may motivate new P1.x and P2.x studies, but will not retroactively alter the
frozen designs or historical interpretation of P1 and P2.

### 3. Relationship to EXP-P1

EXP-P1 asks:

Can validated invariants preserve useful reasoning from a bounded corpus more effectively or
efficiently than alternative representations?

P1 is concerned principally with:

- reasoning preservation;
- context representation;
- compression;
- retrieval and reuse;
- consistency across repeated tasks.

EXP-P2 asks:

Can an invariant-governed generation and verification process reduce the effort required to produce
a structurally valid physical artifact?

P2 is concerned principally with:

- structural validity;
- consequence;
- verification;
- repair;
- readiness;
- contact with physical reality.

Neither experiment can rescue the other.

A positive P2 result cannot be used to convert a failed P1 result into success.

A positive P1 result cannot be used to claim consequence reduction if P2 does not demonstrate it.

### 4. Motivating problem

Large language models can produce outputs that are:

- fluent;
- coherent;
- detailed;
- visually or verbally plausible;
- internally persuasive;

while remaining structurally invalid.

A design may resemble a manufacturable lamp while omitting or violating the conditions required for:

- stability;
- assembly;
- electrical continuity;
- heat management;
- material compatibility;
- maintenance;
- fabrication.

This creates a difference between two forms of apparent success:

Linguistic plausibility

The response resembles an appropriate answer.

Structural validity

The artifact can satisfy the physical conditions necessary for its intended use.

P2 examines the measurable difference between those two states.

The motivating observation may be expressed in plain language as:

A model may understand the request at the level of language while failing to preserve the
conditions required by reality.

This statement is motivational. It is not registered as a claim about a model's internal mental
state or "understanding."

### 5. The invariant object and its task representation

P2 does not define an invariant solely as a sentence in a prompt.

Within the wider programme, an invariant is intended to be a persistent research object with
properties such as:

- identity;
- scope;
- provenance;
- validation evidence;
- explicit satisfaction conditions;
- relationships to other invariants;
- history of application;
- consequence evidence;
- confidence or standing;
- revision status.

For the purposes of P2, these objects will be projected into forms that current language models and
engineering tools can consume.

Those projections may include:

- structured natural language;
- identifiers;
- predicates;
- numerical thresholds;
- equations;
- tables;
- graphs;
- machine-readable schemas;
- test specifications.

P2 does not claim to have discovered the optimal invariant representation.

It tests an initial, auditable representation compatible with contemporary models and external
verification systems.

### 6. Clarification of "pre-paid reasoning"

P2 will not register the claim that invariant projection reduces internal model computation or
prevents the model from reasoning over projected conditions.

The current, narrower interpretation is:

The cost of discovering, validating, formalizing, and maintaining a governing condition has been
borne before the present task, allowing that condition to be reused without being rediscovered and
revalidated from first principles for every new artifact.

This is an epistemic and organizational amortization claim.

P2 does not directly test that amortization because it is principally a single-run consequence
experiment.

A repeated-task experiment would be required to measure whether reusable invariant objects reduce
cumulative discovery and validation cost across multiple novel tasks.

### 7. Primary research question

In physical-design tasks, does an invariant-governed generation and verification pipeline reduce
total expert effort required to reach a physically validated acceptance threshold compared with
content-equivalent prose guidance and an equivalent generic review process?

### 8. Primary hypothesis

Candidate artifacts generated under pre-validated structural conditions and independently verified
against those conditions before release will require less total expert effort to reach the
registered acceptance threshold than artifacts produced using semantically equivalent prose
instructions followed by an equivalent generic review pass.

The load-bearing confirmatory comparison is:

Arm D versus Arm B+R

### 9. Secondary research question

Does expressing semantically equivalent structural content as discrete, identified, independently
testable invariant predicates affect generation quality relative to expressing that content as
ordinary prose instructions?

The secondary confirmatory comparison is:

Arm C versus Arm B

This comparison tests a representation effect within the constraints of contemporary language-model
prompting.

It does not test the complete invariant-system thesis.

A null result for C versus B is compatible with a positive result for D versus B+R.

### 10. Secondary hypotheses

H2.1 — Effort to acceptance

Arm D will reduce total expert effort required to reach the prototype-ready threshold relative to
Arm B+R.

H2.2 — Critical structural failures

Arm D outputs will contain fewer unresolved critical structural violations than Arm B+R outputs
after equalized review.

H2.3 — Out-of-set performance

Arm D will not merely reduce failures explicitly named in the projected invariant set; it will also
avoid or surface a greater proportion of relevant out-of-set failure modes.

H2.4 — Epistemic calibration

Invariant-governed outputs will more accurately distinguish:

- known facts;
- derived values;
- assumptions;
- unresolved measurements;
- prohibited inventions.

H2.5 — Time to value

Arm D will require fewer expert-hours and fewer revision cycles to reach the acceptance threshold.

H2.6 — Representation effect

Arm C will differ from Arm B in one or more generation-stage quality measures under independently
audited content equivalence.

No direction is assumed unless specified following the pilot.

H2.7 — Directed verification effect

Invariant-directed verification will outperform generic review when additional inference passes and
compute are controlled.

H2.8 — Reality-proxy validity

The expert-review and simulation scoring pipeline will correlate sufficiently with outcomes observed
in a physically fabricated subsample.

### 11. Non-claims

P2 does not test or claim:

- that invariant syntax alone is categorically distinct from prompt engineering;
- that a model performs fewer hidden computations when reading invariants;
- that a model literally understands or fails to understand reality;
- that invariants replace engineers or domain experts;
- that structural invariants validate constitutional invariants;
- that all requirements are invariants;
- that all invariants are mathematical fields;
- that the present representation is optimal;
- that success generalizes beyond the tested models, domains, and task conditions;
- that classical verification and validation are obsolete;
- that every element of the pipeline is technically unprecedented;
- that a single image contains enough information for production;
- that P2 validates the entire invariant research programme;
- that a positive result proves a new theory of general intelligence.

### 12. Consequence as a structural scientific outcome

For P2, consequence is defined as:

A measurable state or state transition produced when a generated artifact is evaluated, simulated,
fabricated, assembled, or operated within a specified environment.

Examples include:

- manufacturable or non-manufacturable;
- assemblable or non-assemblable;
- stable or unstable;
- electrically continuous or discontinuous;
- thermally acceptable or unacceptable;
- dimensionally complete or incomplete;
- accepted for prototyping or returned for repair;
- functional or non-functional after fabrication.

The experiment therefore measures the output's fate, not merely its fluency.

### 13. Experimental substrate

#### 13.1 Object classes

The experiment will use at least three physical-design classes representing different structural
regimes.

The initial candidates are:

Object class 1 — Decorative electrical lamp

Primary regimes:

- visual interpretation;
- stability;
- electrical continuity;
- heat;
- manufacturability;
- assembly;
- maintenance access.

Object class 2 — Wall-mounted shelf bracket or small load-bearing fixture

Primary regimes:

- load path;
- material strength;
- fastening;
- wall interface;
- deformation;
- installation;
- safety factor.

Object class 3 — Ventilated electronics enclosure

Primary regimes:

- component fit;
- thermal management;
- cable routing;
- ventilation;
- assembly;
- access;
- manufacturability;
- electrical isolation.

Final object selection will be reviewed by independent domain experts before registration.

The tasks should be sufficiently challenging to create meaningful structural failures without
requiring industrial-scale fabrication.

#### 13.2 Information-sufficiency conditions

Each object class will be crossed with two input conditions.

Condition I — Underdetermined input

The model receives:

- one or more photographs or standardized renders;
- intended use;
- limited contextual information.

This condition measures epistemic restraint and the handling of missing information.

Condition II — Standardized measurement pack

The model receives:

- the same visual material;
- defined dimensions;
- material information where appropriate;
- intended loads or operating conditions;
- component requirements;
- relevant environmental assumptions.

This condition reduces information insufficiency and places greater weight on structural reasoning
and artifact composition.

The experiment will not treat refusal to invent absent measurements as failure.

However, an output that merely defers all work will incur the expert effort required to complete the
missing specification.

### 14. Experimental arms

Arm A — Plain generation

The model receives:

- the task materials;
- the intended use;
- a standard request to produce the required design package.

No additional structural guidance or dedicated review pass is provided.

Arm A establishes the practical baseline.

It does not isolate a single mechanism.

Arm B — Content-matched prose guidance

The model receives the same task and the same substantive structural information used in Arm C.

The content is expressed as ordinary prose guidance or engineering instructions.

Illustrative form:

- ensure the object is stable under the specified use conditions;
- ensure all parts can be manufactured and assembled;
- account for the electrical, thermal, and maintenance requirements;
- identify any information that cannot be inferred.

The final Arm B text will be independently audited for semantic equivalence with Arm C.

Arm C — Testable invariant representation

The model receives the same substantive content as Arm B, expressed as discrete invariant objects or
projections.

Each projected condition will include, where applicable:

- invariant identifier;
- scope;
- condition;
- test or satisfaction rule;
- numerical limit or threshold;
- dependencies;
- declared uncertainty;
- required evidence.

Illustrative form:

INV-MECH-STAB-01

Scope: freestanding object under registered operating conditions.

Condition: the projected centre of mass must remain within the defined support boundary, including
the registered stability margin.

Test: calculate or estimate centre-of-mass position and support geometry; return pass, fail, or
unresolved.

Arm C receives no dedicated post-generation review pass.

C versus B tests representation-at-generation under content-matched conditions.

Arm B+R — Prose guidance plus generic review

The model receives the same conditions as Arm B.

After producing an initial candidate, it receives an additional generic review instruction and
equivalent review budget.

Illustrative review instruction:

Review the complete proposed design for errors, omissions, inconsistencies, impracticalities, and
foreseeable failure modes. Correct the design before returning the final package.

The generic review is not supplied with invariant identifiers, registered tests, or a
condition-by-condition verification procedure.

Arm B+R controls for:

- second-pass reasoning;
- additional token use;
- additional processing time;
- generic self-critique;
- opportunity to revise.

Arm D — Invariant-governed generation and directed verification

The model receives the same projected invariant set as Arm C.

After candidate generation, the artifact enters an explicit verification process.

The process must:

1. enumerate every registered invariant;
2. test the candidate artifact against each condition;
3. return pass, fail, or unresolved;
4. provide the evidence used;
5. identify conflicts between conditions;
6. distinguish missing information from genuine violation;
7. record repairs;
8. rerun affected tests after repair;
9. withhold prototype-ready status while critical conditions remain failed or unresolved.

The final output includes:

- the design package;
- a machine-readable verification record;
- unresolved issues;
- declared readiness state;
- traceability from each condition to evidence.

D versus B+R is the primary confirmatory comparison.

### 15. Optional external-verifier implementation

The final protocol should determine whether Arm D verification is performed by:

- the same model in a separately isolated pass;
- a separate model;
- deterministic engineering tools;
- a hybrid process.

A preferred implementation may combine:

1. model-based interpretation;
2. deterministic checks where possible;
3. expert adjudication;
4. simulation outputs.

Where the same model performs generation and verification, the limitations must be declared.

Where a separate model is used, model identity and role must be preregistered.

The verification process must not have access to the hidden arm labels used by evaluators.

### 16. Content-equivalence controls

The B/C comparison is valid only if the substantive content is held constant.

Before generation:

1. Arm B and Arm C materials will be independently compared.
2. Auditors will determine whether every substantive condition in C is present in B.
3. No additional engineering knowledge may appear only in C.
4. Numerical thresholds must be present in both conditions where needed.
5. Token counts will be held within a preregistered tolerance.
6. Ordering effects will be controlled or randomized where feasible.
7. Output requirements will be identical.
8. Model, sampling parameters, context materials, and tool access will be held constant.

Auditors will not be members of the prompt-authoring or scoring teams.

The equivalence audit will be hash-committed before the run.

### 17. Compute and review controls

B+R and D must receive comparable opportunities for review.

The protocol will preregister:

- maximum review passes;
- token or compute budget;
- time budget where applicable;
- tool access;
- stopping conditions;
- revision count;
- model identity;
- sampling parameters.

If invariant-directed verification requires materially different external tooling, the distinction
will be reported as part of the pipeline effect rather than concealed as a pure prompting comparison.

The primary claim concerns the complete pipeline, so tool-enabled differences may be legitimate.
Their contribution must nevertheless be measured and disclosed.

### 18. Invariant-set construction

Candidate structural invariants may be derived from:

- accepted engineering principles;
- relevant standards;
- specialist design knowledge;
- manufacturing requirements;
- documented failure modes;
- physical constraints;
- validated prior artifacts.

The invariant set must not be generated solely by the same model being evaluated.

Each candidate must specify:

- identifier;
- natural-language description;
- formal or numerical condition where possible;
- domain and scope;
- evidence basis;
- test procedure;
- known exceptions;
- dependency relationships;
- unresolved uncertainty.

The set will be frozen before any confirmatory generation.

### 19. Independent validation

An independent panel of suitably qualified engineers will review the candidate invariant set.

The panel must have no role in:

- writing arm prompts;
- generating outputs;
- designing the scoring rubric;
- scoring experimental outputs.

The panel will assess:

- correctness;
- relevance;
- necessity;
- testability;
- scope;
- ambiguity;
- redundancy;
- conflicts;
- completeness within the declared domain.

Validation does not imply that the set is complete.

The approved set will be hash-committed before generation.

Any later addition is exploratory and cannot be inserted into confirmatory scoring retrospectively.

### 20. In-set and out-of-set failures

The evaluation taxonomy must include two classes.

In-set failures

Failures directly governed by projected invariants.

Out-of-set failures

Relevant structural failures not explicitly covered by the projected set.

This distinction is critical.

If Arm D improves only on in-set conditions, the result may indicate successful rubric matching or
directed compliance.

If it also improves on out-of-set conditions, the result may support a broader structural benefit,
though alternative explanations must still be considered.

Out-of-set discoveries may inform later invariant research but may not alter the frozen P2
conditions.

### 21. Primary outcome

The primary outcome is:

Total independent expert effort required to transform the raw experimental output into an artifact
package that reaches the registered prototype-ready acceptance threshold.

Effort may be measured in standardized expert-minutes, expert-hours, or another preregistered unit.

The measure includes time required to:

- identify failures;
- obtain missing information;
- correct invented details;
- redesign invalid components;
- complete omitted specifications;
- resolve contradictions;
- rebuild CAD or drawings;
- repeat calculations;
- perform simulations;
- prepare the accepted package.

This measure deliberately prices both:

- overconfident invention; and
- unhelpful under-commitment.

### 22. Acceptance threshold

The acceptance threshold will be defined before generation.

A prototype-ready artifact package may require:

- complete registered dimensions or explicit measurement requests;
- specified materials;
- manufacturable component geometry;
- assembly feasibility;
- defined interfaces and tolerances;
- structural adequacy;
- electrical adequacy where applicable;
- thermal adequacy where applicable;
- maintenance or access provisions;
- a bill of materials;
- assembly sequence;
- critical risks and unresolved items;
- readiness status accurately declared.

The threshold may vary appropriately by object class but must remain consistent across arms within
each class.

The threshold will be designed by domain experts who do not score arm identity.

### 23. Secondary outcomes

Secondary measures will include:

- number of critical, major, and minor violations;
- in-set violations;
- out-of-set violations;
- unsupported material claims;
- missing mandatory specifications;
- correctly disclosed unknowns;
- incorrectly declared readiness;
- revision cycles;
- expert disagreement;
- token usage;
- tool usage;
- compute cost;
- generation time;
- review time;
- simulation performance;
- fabrication success;
- aesthetic fidelity where relevant;
- number of novel failure modes discovered.

Counterfactual statements such as "this might have caused a fire" will be exploratory only and
excluded from headline findings unless directly supported by registered evidence.

### 24. Failure-severity taxonomy

A preregistered taxonomy will classify failures as:

Critical

A failure that:

- prevents intended function;
- creates a material safety hazard;
- makes fabrication or assembly impossible;
- invalidates the declared readiness state.

Major

A failure requiring substantive redesign, replacement of key components, or significant additional
engineering work.

Minor

A correctable defect that does not require substantive redesign.

Each output will be independently rated by multiple evaluators.

Disagreements will be resolved using a preregistered adjudication process.

### 25. Evaluator blinding and output normalization

Invariant-guided outputs may reveal their arm through style, identifiers, caution language, or
structure.

Before expert scoring:

1. outputs will be normalized into a common artifact format;
2. arm-specific metadata will be removed;
3. invariant identifiers and review labels will be hidden where possible;
4. content necessary for evaluating the artifact will be preserved;
5. normalization will be performed by a party not involved in scoring;
6. evaluators will record their guessed arm after scoring;
7. evaluator-arm guess accuracy will be reported as a blinding diagnostic.

Some machine-readable traces may need separate evaluation. These will not be shown during blinded
artifact scoring unless required by a specific registered measure.

### 26. Evaluation roles

The following functions should be organizationally separated:

1. theory and experiment design;
2. invariant authorship;
3. invariant validation;
4. prose/invariant equivalence audit;
5. output generation;
6. output normalization;
7. blinded structural scoring;
8. repair-to-threshold execution;
9. statistical analysis;
10. fabrication;
11. constitutional and scientific-integrity review.

Individuals may hold more than one role only where unavoidable, and any overlap must be declared
before the run.

### 27. Sample structure

The final sample size will be set after a pilot and power analysis.

The intended design includes:

- at least three object classes;
- two information-sufficiency conditions;
- five experimental arms;
- at least two model classes;
- repeated independent generations per cell.

An illustrative lower-bound structure is:

3 object classes × 2 information conditions × 5 arms × 2 models × 10 generations

This would yield 600 generated artifacts before exclusions.

The final number may differ based on:

- pilot variance;
- expert-evaluation capacity;
- fabrication cost;
- expected effect;
- model stochasticity;
- statistical power.

No final sample size will be chosen solely because it produces a desired result.

### 28. Models and reproducibility

The experiment should use at least two model classes or providers where operationally possible.

The protocol will record:

- model name and version;
- provider;
- date;
- system instructions;
- sampling parameters;
- context window;
- tool access;
- all prompts;
- all outputs;
- review passes;
- errors and retries;
- seed where supported;
- token and compute measures where available.

Per-model results will be reported.

Aggregate results must not conceal model-specific reversals.

### 29. Physical ground-truth anchor

Expert review and simulation remain proxies for physical consequence.

A preregistered subsample will therefore be physically fabricated or constructed.

The subsample should include outputs from multiple arms and be selected using a method that does not
favor the hypothesis.

Fabrication will test, where applicable:

- component manufacturability;
- dimensional fit;
- assembly path;
- stability;
- load behavior;
- electrical continuity;
- thermal behavior;
- maintenance access;
- functional operation.

The physical results will be compared with:

- expert predictions;
- simulation results;
- registered invariant tests;
- declared readiness.

This will estimate whether the scalable review pipeline is a valid proxy for reality.

### 30. Simulation and deterministic checks

Where feasible, outputs will be translated into standardized CAD or machine-readable engineering
artifacts.

Possible checks include:

- mass properties;
- centre-of-mass envelope;
- support geometry;
- simplified load and deformation analysis;
- collision and interference checks;
- assembly-path analysis;
- cable routing;
- electrical connectivity;
- component clearance;
- airflow and thermal approximation;
- tolerance completeness.

Not all conditions will be reducible to deterministic tests.

The protocol must distinguish:

- machine-verifiable conditions;
- simulation-dependent conditions;
- expert-judgment conditions;
- fabrication-dependent conditions.

### 31. Confirmatory contrasts

The primary confirmatory contrast is:

D versus B+R

This tests the complete invariant-governed pipeline against content-equivalent prose guidance with
generic review and equivalent review opportunity.

The secondary confirmatory contrast is:

C versus B

This tests whether invariant-form representation affects generation under strict content matching.

Additional contrasts are exploratory unless preregistered:

- B versus A;
- C versus A;
- B+R versus B;
- D versus C;
- D versus A;
- interactions by domain;
- interactions by model;
- interactions by information sufficiency.

Multiple-comparison controls will be defined in the analysis plan.

### 32. Meaningful effect

The final effect threshold will be set after pilot variance is known and before the confirmatory
run.

A candidate practical success threshold is:

### 34. Success interpretations

Full primary success

Representation and pipeline success

Verification-only success

### 35. Partial and null interpretations

Information-only result

Generic-review result

Representation-only result

Broad null result

- the verification process adds no value;
- the instruments are too coarse;

A null result will not automatically justify rerunning the experiment with changed criteria.

### 36. Adverse result

An adverse result occurs if invariant-guided conditions:

- increase critical failures;
- increase total expert effort;
- create false confidence;
- suppress relevant out-of-set concerns;
- cause premature readiness declarations;
- distort the artifact around an incomplete invariant set.

An adverse result will be preserved and reported.

The affected claim will be halted pending redesign and independent review.

### 37. Falsification conditions

The primary invariant-governed pipeline claim is not supported if, under the registered conditions:

- D does not materially outperform B+R;
- D increases expert effort or critical failures;
- observed improvements are fully explained by unequal information, compute, tools, or evaluator
  unblinding;
- benefits do not reproduce within the declared minimum domain and model scope;
- the physical ground-truth subsample contradicts the expert-and-simulation findings;
- the verification process merely optimizes to in-set conditions while worsening consequential
  out-of-set failures.

The secondary representation claim is not supported if:

- C does not materially differ from B under audited semantic equivalence;
- any apparent difference disappears after normalization or blinding correction;
- the difference is attributable to unmatched specificity, ordering, or token allocation.

### 38. Programme-level stopping rule

The broad claim that invariant systems provide demonstrable value beyond ordinary context provision,
prompt engineering, and generic review will remain unsupported if adequately powered and
independently evaluated studies show the following pattern:

1. P1 demonstrates no reproducible preservation, compression, or efficiency advantage over matched
   controls;
2. P2 demonstrates no reduction in effort-to-threshold or critical structural failure relative to
   content- and compute-matched controls;
3. future repeated-task research demonstrates no cumulative reuse or amortization advantage;
4. adverse effects equal or exceed observed benefits.

If this pattern emerges, the programme will concede that the tested invariant architecture has not
demonstrated its claimed distinct value.

Specific sub-findings may remain valid, such as:

- structured prompting helps;
- verification helps;
- domain knowledge helps;
- engineering review helps.

Those findings must not be relabeled as evidence for an unsupported invariant-specific claim.

### 43. Revised scientific framing

P2 begins with the distinction that:

Linguistic plausibility and structural validity are not the same outcome.

It tests the proposition that:

A generated artifact should be evaluated not only by the answer it presents, but by the effort and
failure involved in making that answer valid in reality.

The primary experiment is not:

Does invariant wording improve a prompt?

It is:

Does a system that projects validated structural conditions, generates under them, verifies against
them, and withholds readiness until material violations are resolved produce artifacts that require
less expert work to become physically valid?

### 46. Final formulation

P1 asks whether invariants can carry reasoning.

P2 asks whether an invariant-governed process can reduce the cost of turning inference into valid
action.

The revised P2 hypothesis is:

In physical-design tasks, a pipeline that projects pre-validated and independently testable
structural conditions, generates under those conditions, verifies candidate artifacts against them,
and withholds readiness until material violations are resolved will reduce total expert effort
required to reach a physically validated acceptance threshold, relative to semantically equivalent
prose guidance followed by an equivalent generic review process.

The result will be judged by what the artifact requires before reality will accept it.
