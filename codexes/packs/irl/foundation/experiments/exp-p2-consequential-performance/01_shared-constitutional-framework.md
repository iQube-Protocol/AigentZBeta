## Part I — What is shared, and why

The operator ruling, verbatim:

> "P2A and P2B should share everything above the experimental domain": constitutional principles ·
> claims discipline · representation certification · statistical analysis · decision procedure ·
> audit framework · information equivalence — *"exactly the same"* for each. **"The only thing
> that changes is the domain."**

The constitutional question the framework serves — unchanged from monolithic P2:

> "Do invariant-guided representations improve consequential task performance compared with
> conventional documentation under equivalent informational content?"

**Reading rule.** Each of the seven sections below states either (a) what already binds, with its
source, or (b) that it is PENDING OPERATOR PROTOCOL. There is no third category. A section that
says "pending" is not an invitation for an agent to fill it in; it is a record of what the
operator will supply.

## Part II — The seven shared concerns

### §1 Constitutional principles

**Status: PENDING OPERATOR PROTOCOL.** The enumerated constitutional principles of the consequence
family are the operator's to state. EXP-P3 carries eight Constitutional Principles in its protocol
(`../exp-p3-representation-of-structural-invariants/02_experimental-protocol.md`); whether the
consequence family adopts, adapts, or replaces them is a protocol decision, not a scaffolding one.

The nearest existing statement of scientific position for a consequence experiment is §3 and §27
of the prior physical protocol (`../exp-p2b-physical-consequences/01_prior-protocol-v1.0-candidate.md`).
It is recorded as prior art, not promoted to family-level principle here.

### §2 Claims discipline

**Status: BINDING NOW.** Two rules already govern every statement made about this family, and
neither waits on the protocol:

1. **Hypothesis vs Canon.** Every P2 hypothesis is an empirical claim under test. It enters and
   remains `proposed` until the registered decision procedure produces supporting evidence. No P2
   claim may be stated as established fact, in this repo or in any external document.
2. **Scope of a positive result.** A positive result supports only the evaluated workflow — never
   "the complete invariant framework", the Discovery Engine, or Constitutional Computing as a
   whole. This is the discipline the prior physical protocol states at §28, generalised to the
   family.

RSS-001 extends the same discipline to *methods*: "a procedure that has never been executed is a
proposal, not a method" (`RSS-001 §6`). Any method this family adopts and has not executed is
described as adopted, not as validated.

### §3 Representation certification — the RSS-001 admissibility gate

**Status: BINDING NOW as a PRECONDITION. The parameters it requires frozen are PENDING OPERATOR
PROTOCOL.**

The ruling calls this the biggest methodological change:

> "The original P2 protocol assumed that the representations were 'equivalent.' **P3 teaches us we
> should never assume that.** Instead every representation entering P2A or P2B should first pass
> RSS-001 certification: Atomic Content Mapping · Informational Equivalence · Tiered Computational
> Equivalence · Representation Certification · Assumption Back-Propagation. **Only then is it
> admissible into the consequence experiment.**"

The gate is a **precondition of admissibility**, not an outcome measure: a representation that has
not passed certification does not enter the consequence experiment at all. The standard is
RSS-001, resident with EXP-P3 at
`../exp-p3-representation-of-structural-invariants/03_RSS-001_representation-science-standard.md`
and explicitly reusable ("a **reusable standard, not a P3-only document**").

The five named steps resolve to these RSS-001 sections:

| Ruling's step | RSS-001 section | What it requires |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic Content Mapping | `RSS-001 §1.1.1` | Decompose each invariant into atomic structural conditions; a content-mapping matrix records, per condition per representation, whether it is present, how encoded, explicit or implicit, whether translation required an added assumption, and whether information was lost or introduced. Hash-committed before runs. |
| Informational Equivalence | `RSS-001 §1.1` and `RSS-001 §1.1.2` | The two-part auditable standard, completed by an independent back-translation audit: reviewers see one representation at a time and reconstruct its structural conditions against the canonical atomic-condition set. Back-translation performance is the operational certificate of informational comparability. |
| Tiered Computational Equivalence | `RSS-001 §2`, with `RSS-001 §T.2`, `RSS-001 §T.3`, `RSS-001 §T.4`, `RSS-001 §T.5` | The two-tier claim architecture — Tier 1 substrate *access* effect and Tier 2 reasoning-*given*-access effect, separated by the extraction gate (`RSS-001 §T.4`) and governed by the Tier 2 eligibility rules (`RSS-001 §T.5`). Both tiers are confirmatory; neither may be reported as the other. |
| Representation Certification | `RSS-001 §1` | The whole certification chain: atomic decomposition → content mapping → assumption back-propagation → canonical assumption closure → informational equivalence audit → back translation → certification. "Only certified materials enter confirmatory analysis." |
| Assumption Back-Propagation | `RSS-001 §3`, with the procedure at `RSS-001 §BP.3` and the normative worked example at `RSS-001 §BP.8` | Content commitments forced by one representation propagate to the others; encoding properties do not. That distinction is load-bearing — naive propagation would dissolve the manipulation. |
| Structural fidelity — how it may be measured | `RSS-001 §1.2` | Structural fidelity is measured through the content and back-translation audits, never inferred from downstream task performance. That inference would be circular, and it is the exact circularity a consequence experiment is most tempted by. |

**Instantiation obligations.** Adopting RSS-001 is not a citation; `RSS-001 §6` sets what an
adopting experiment must do: register its representation families; freeze every ⟦ ⟧ parameter with
independent justification; bind the standard's "Section DP" references to its own preregistered
statistical analysis plan; and cite the standard by version. For this family, all four are
**PENDING OPERATOR PROTOCOL** — with one consequence that must be stated plainly: **until the
extraction-gate thresholds are frozen and the parameters registered, no representation is
certified, and therefore no representation is yet admissible into P2A or P2B.** The gate is
declared; it has not yet been passed by anything.

`RSS-001 §6` also fixes versioning: a running experiment stays bound to the RSS-001 version it
froze, and no amendment may be motivated by an in-flight experiment's observed results.

### §4 Statistical analysis

**Status: PENDING OPERATOR PROTOCOL.** Shared across P2A and P2B by the ruling. Nothing is
specified here — no endpoints, no power analysis, no model, no thresholds. EXP-P3's plan
(`../exp-p3-representation-of-structural-invariants/04_statistical-analysis-plan.md`) is the
nearest structural precedent in the programme and is named as a precedent only.

### §5 Decision procedure

**Status: PENDING OPERATOR PROTOCOL.** RSS-001 references a "Section DP" that resolves to the
instantiating experiment's statistical analysis plan (`RSS-001 §6`), and the Tier 2 architecture
(`RSS-001 §T.3`) evaluates its outcomes under that procedure. The binding target for this family
does not exist yet; until it does, the RSS-001 references to Section DP are unbound and no
confirmatory claim can be evaluated.

### §6 Audit framework

**Status: ADOPTED from RSS-001; the commitment inventory is PENDING OPERATOR PROTOCOL.**

`RSS-001 §5` is the consolidated audit index, and the family adopts it whole: role separation; the
load-bearing sequencing (canonical decomposition → task corpus committed → arm authoring →
assumption harvest and classification → propagation → canonical assumption closure → closure
freeze → final projection → content map → certification → back-translation → extraction gate →
confirmatory runs); content mapping; back-translation; extraction gating; hash commitments in the
declared dependency order; freeze rules; and escalation. Two freeze rules bear repeating because a
consequence experiment is where they are most likely to be bent: no post-freeze propagation, and
**no post hoc gate adjustment** (`RSS-001 §T.5`) — if pilot data show the candidate thresholds
exclude a substrate, that exclusion is itself reported, and the threshold is not lowered to admit
it.

What is pending: the family's own preregistration bundle and commitment inventory, which
`RSS-001 §5` requires the instantiating experiment to supply.

### §7 Information equivalence

**Status: BINDING NOW as the standard; its per-experiment audit is PENDING OPERATOR PROTOCOL.**

The constitutional question conditions everything on "equivalent informational content", so this
concern is what makes the question answerable at all. The standard is `RSS-001 §1.1` — explicitly
*not* a claim that cross-modal representations are semantically identical, but a two-part
auditable standard: the content-mapping matrix (`RSS-001 §1.1.1`) plus the independent
back-translation audit (`RSS-001 §1.1.2`), scored against the frozen closure. A representation
that fails the preregistered equivalence threshold cannot support a confirmatory substrate-effect
claim.

The prior physical protocol already carried an information-equivalence clause (generation
equivalence and review equivalence, hash committed). The ruling's upgrade is that equivalence is
no longer *asserted by auditors comparing two documents* but *certified by a standard with a
measured operational certificate*. That is the substance of "P3 teaches us we should never assume
that."

## Part III — What the domain changes

Only the consequence domain differs between instantiations. Each instantiation document carries,
for its own domain and nothing else:

- the domain research question, as stated in the ruling;
- the consequence domain and its task/artifact class — **operator's examples, pending protocol**;
- the outcome list for that domain — **operator's examples, pending protocol**;
- the experimental arms for that domain — **the ruling's labels, pending protocol**;
- everything else: a reference back to this framework.

An instantiation that finds itself needing to state a *shared* concern differently has found
either a defect in this framework or a reason the concern is not in fact shared. Both are
operator decisions. Neither is resolved by writing it down twice.
