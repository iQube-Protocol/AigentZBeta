# PART A — Experiment Specification EXP-P3 (v1.0 Candidate)

Invariant Research Lab (IRL)
Experiment Specification
EXP-P3 — Representation of Structural Invariants
Experiment ID: IRL-EXP-P3
Version: 1.0 Candidate
Status: Protocol for External Scientific Review
Classification: Internal (Pending External Review)

## Abstract

Structural knowledge can be represented through multiple substrates, including natural language, mathematical notation, diagrams, and machine-readable structural representations. These representations are commonly treated as interchangeable provided they communicate equivalent information.

EXP-P3 investigates whether this assumption is empirically justified.

The experiment tests whether representational substrate constitutes an independent computational variable under conditions of audited informational equivalence. Structural knowledge is held constant while representational substrate is systematically varied across a preregistered set of representation families. Differences in computational performance are evaluated using representation-independent ground truth and controlled inferential procedures.

The experiment deliberately makes no claim that any existing representational substrate is privileged or native to structural invariants. Rather, it seeks to determine whether representation itself exerts measurable computational consequences.

## 1. Scientific Objective

To determine whether representational substrate materially affects computational reasoning over structurally equivalent knowledge.

## 2. Primary Research Question

Under conditions of audited informational equivalence, does representational substrate materially affect computational reasoning?

## 3. Secondary Questions

1. Does representational substrate affect accessibility independently of reasoning?
2. Do representational effects vary across different classes of reasoning task?
3. Do hybrid representations provide complementary reasoning advantages?
4. Can informational equivalence be certified independently of representational substrate?

## 4. Scientific Scope

This experiment investigates representation.

It does not investigate: invariant discovery; reasoning compression; consequential decision quality; interaction between invariants; human cognition; representation learning.

Those questions belong to other experiments within the Constitutional Cybernetics programme.

## 5. Relationship to the Research Programme

| Experiment | Focus |
|---|---|
| EXP-P1 | Reasoning Compression |
| EXP-P2 | Consequential Performance |
| EXP-P3 | Representation |
| EXP-P4 (Reserved) | Interaction |

Each experiment is independently interpretable.

## 6. Hypotheses

**Primary Hypothesis (H3.1)** — Representational substrate is an independent computational variable.

**Secondary Hypotheses** —
H3.2: Representational effects differ by task regime.
H3.3: Accessibility varies independently of downstream reasoning performance.
H3.4: Controlled hybrid representations demonstrate complementary strengths not exhibited by constituent representations alone.

## 7. Experimental Design

Controlled representation comparison.

Independent Variable: Representational substrate.

Dependent Variables — Tier 1: Accessibility. Tier 2: Reasoning performance.

## 8. Representation Families

| Arm | Representation |
|---|---|
| L | Natural Language |
| M | Mathematical Representation |
| D | Diagrammatic Representation |
| S | Serialized Structural Representation |
| H | Controlled Hybrid Representation |

## 9. Experimental Principle

The experiment attempts to satisfy the following condition: Structural knowledge remains constant. Representation alone varies.

Any detected performance differences are therefore attributable to representation rather than informational content.

## 10. Content Certification

All experimental materials undergo certification before use.

Certification consists of: Atomic Content decomposition; Content Mapping; Assumption Back-Propagation; Canonical Assumption Closure; Informational Equivalence Audit; Back Translation; Representation Certification.

Only certified materials enter confirmatory analysis.

## 11. Task Regimes

The experiment evaluates performance across multiple reasoning regimes, including: Spatial; Quantitative; Logical; Procedural; Contextual.

Tasks are preregistered before representation-specific materials are authored.

## 12. Ground Truth

Ground truth remains independent of representation.

Permitted mechanisms include: simulation; formal proof; engineering verification; validated reference solutions.

No representation serves as ground truth for another.

## 13. Tiered Evaluation Architecture

**Tier 1** — Measures representational accessibility. Question: Can the representation be reliably interpreted? Tier 1 findings constitute legitimate scientific outcomes.

**Tier 2** — Measures reasoning performance after successful accessibility. Tier 2 evaluates reasoning conditional upon Tier 1 eligibility.

Tier 1 and Tier 2 claims remain explicitly separated throughout analysis.

## 14. Decision Procedure

Inferential decisions follow the preregistered hierarchical decision procedure.

The protocol defines: confirmatory analyses; exploratory analyses; equivalence testing; interaction testing; falsification conditions; supported null conditions; claim eligibility.

No confirmatory claims may be made outside the registered decision framework.

## 15. Outcome Measures

Primary: Structural-validity reasoning accuracy.
Co-primary: Contradiction rate.
Secondary: extraction accuracy; uncertainty; omitted conditions; invented conditions; reconstruction fidelity; consistency; computational efficiency (descriptive only).

## 16. Claims Discipline

The experiment may conclude: representation affects reasoning; representation affects accessibility; effects vary by task regime; hybrid representations demonstrate complementary behaviour.

The experiment may not conclude: mathematics is the language of invariants; language is inadequate; diagrams are superior generally; structural invariants possess a native representation; interaction or field behaviour exists; new representational systems have been discovered.

## 17. Internal Validity Controls

The protocol incorporates controls including: informational equivalence certification; assumption back-propagation; canonical closure; independent authorship; independent auditing; role separation; back translation; extraction validation; preregistered statistical procedures.

These controls are intended to isolate representational substrate as the primary experimental variable.

## 18. Expected Scientific Contributions

1. Evidence regarding the computational role of representation.
2. A preregistered methodology for comparing representational substrates under audited informational equivalence.
3. Reusable methods for representation certification, assumption back-propagation, and content equivalence auditing.

## 19. Success Criteria

The experiment is considered successful if it produces a scientifically interpretable result, regardless of whether that result supports or rejects the primary hypothesis. Both positive findings and supported null results are regarded as meaningful contributions.

## 20. Limitations

Results apply only to: the registered representation families; the registered notation systems; the tested model architectures; the preregistered task corpus.

No broader claims are made beyond these conditions.

## 21. Future Research

Should representational effects be demonstrated, subsequent investigations may explore: representational optimization; adaptive structural representations; interaction between structural invariants; representational dynamics.

These investigations fall outside the scope of EXP-P3.

**Aletheon's structuring recommendation:** make this the constitutional specification for P3 and keep all implementation detail — Tier decision trees, BP procedures, visual notation specification, statistical plans, audit workflows — in separate appendices, mirroring how complex engineering standards are written: Main Specification (~20–25 pages, the scientific contract) · Appendix A: Statistical Analysis Plan (DP) · Appendix B: Tier 1/Tier 2 Evaluation Framework · Appendix C: Assumption Back-Propagation Protocol (BP) · Appendix D: Visual Notation Specification (VN) · Appendix E: Audit & Certification Procedures · Appendix F: Materials, task corpus, and implementation details.

# PART C — IRL Internal Research Record: EXP-P3

Document ID: IRL-EXP-P3-RR-001
Version: v1.0 (Internal Draft)
Classification: Internal Research Record (IRL Confidential)
Status: Living Design History (Frozen upon commencement of experiment)

## Purpose

This document records the intellectual development of EXP-P3. Unlike the experimental specification, which defines what will be tested, this document records how the experiment evolved, the reasoning behind major design decisions, alternative hypotheses considered, reviewer feedback, and the evolution of the underlying scientific question. It is intended to preserve the provenance of the experiment for future researchers and for the historical record of the Invariant Research Lab (IRL).

## 1. Position Within the Research Programme

| Experiment | Primary Question | Scientific Domain |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Can reasoning be compressed into structural invariants? | Compression |
| P2 | Do invariant-guided workflows improve consequential outcomes? | Consequence |
| P3 | Does representational substrate materially affect reasoning? | Representation |
| P4 (Reserved) | Do structural invariants exhibit interaction or field-like behaviour? | Interaction |

P3 represents the third foundational experiment. Unlike P1 and P2, it investigates not structural knowledge itself but the computational consequences of its representation.

## 2. Origin of the Experiment

**Initial Observation.** During development of P1 and P2, repeated discussions arose concerning the apparent superiority of different representational systems: mathematics; engineering diagrams; symbolic logic; structured schemas; natural language. Initially these discussions centred on questions such as: "Is mathematics the language of structural invariants?" This framing was attractive but ultimately rejected because it embedded an implicit conclusion within the research question.

**Reframing.** The programme instead separated two distinct concepts: Information and Representation. The revised question became: "Does representational substrate materially affect computational reasoning over equivalent structural knowledge?" This reframing transformed the experiment from a search for a privileged language into an investigation of representation itself as an independent scientific variable. This became the defining hypothesis of P3.

## 3. Evolution of the Scientific Question

Stage 1 — Mathematical Primacy. Working assumption: mathematics may constitute the natural representation of structural invariants. Assessment: rejected as prematurely privileging one representational system.

Stage 2 — Language versus Mathematics. Question: do mathematical formulations outperform equivalent prose? Assessment: recognized as too narrow; additional representational systems required.

Stage 3 — Representation Taxonomy. Expansion to multiple candidate substrates: Language; Mathematics; Diagrammatic Representation; Serialized Structural Representation; Controlled Hybrid Representation. Representation itself became the manipulated variable.

Stage 4 — Representation versus Information. Recognition that previous comparisons often conflated informational differences with representational differences. This produced the central design objective: hold information constant; vary representation only.

Stage 5 — Computational Equivalence. Recognition that informational equivalence alone was insufficient. Representations also differed in accessibility. This produced the distinction between informational equivalence and computational equivalence.

Stage 6 — Tiered Claims. Accessibility became recognised as a legitimate experimental outcome. The protocol therefore separated Tier 1 (accessibility — representation can be read) from Tier 2 (reasoning once representation has been successfully interpreted). This resolved the "parseability paradox" identified during review.

## 4. Major Conceptual Inflection Points

**Information is not Representation.** Perhaps the single most important insight. Equivalent information may exist in computationally different forms. Therefore: representation is itself experimentally manipulable.

**Accessibility is Part of Computation.** Failure to interpret a representation is not experimental noise. It is itself a computational property. This led directly to the Tier 1/Tier 2 architecture.

**Representation Requires Certification.** The programme rejected simple assertions of "equivalent content." Instead it introduced explicit certification through: Atomic Content decomposition; Content Mapping; Back Translation; Assumption Back-Propagation; Canonical Closure.

**Representation Must Not Collapse.** Early back-propagation proposals unintentionally homogenized all representations. The resulting insight: Propagate content. Preserve encoding. This became one of the central methodological principles of P3.

## 5. Reviewer History

**Internal Design.** Initial experimental concept. Primary emphasis: language versus mathematics.

**Venice Review — Round 1.** Outcome: major redesign required. Principal findings included: representation ≠ invariant; informational equivalence required formal treatment; Arm S required; extraction validation required; task taxonomy required; representation-independent ground truth required; stronger claims discipline required. The review fundamentally reshaped the experiment.

**Internal Revision.** Substantial restructuring. Key additions included: Arm S; Representation taxonomy; Ground truth independence; Common output schema; Claims discipline.

**Venice Review — Round 2.** Assessment: architecture considered scientifically sound. Remaining work shifted from conceptual architecture toward protocol engineering. Major additions included: Tier 1 / Tier 2 claim structure; Hierarchical decision procedure; Assumption Back-Propagation; Canonical Assumption Closure; Visual Notation Specification; Audit sequencing; Role separation; Diagrammatic neutrality. At this point the experiment transitioned from conceptual design into preregistration engineering.

## 6. Major Design Decisions

**Why Arm S Exists** — To distinguish explicit structural representation from visual modality. Without Arm S, differences between diagrams and prose could not be separated from differences between visual and structural encoding.

**Why Tier 1 Exists** — Accessibility forms part of computational equivalence. Failure to parse a representation constitutes a legitimate scientific finding rather than an exclusion criterion.

**Why Assumption Back-Propagation Exists** — Representations inevitably introduce additional assumptions. Those assumptions must either propagate to every representation, or be excluded from confirmatory analysis. Simple declaration proved insufficient.

**Why Canonical Assumption Closure Exists** — Naïve propagation creates recursive assumption generation. Closure computation produces a stable fixed point while preserving representational diversity.

**Why Visual Notation Was Formalized** — Diagrammatic representations otherwise become dependent upon individual authors. The notation therefore became an experimental instrument requiring specification and calibration.

**Why P4 Was Deferred** — Interaction, interference, resonance and field behaviour concern properties of structural invariants themselves rather than their representation. Accordingly these hypotheses were separated into a future experiment.

## 7. Rejected Alternatives

The following approaches were considered and deliberately rejected: Mathematics as privileged representation. Natural language as default representation. Infinite recursive assumption propagation. Universal diagram notation. Token efficiency as primary outcome. Performance-defined representation discovery. Hybrid-first experimental design. Representation inferred from model preference. Field interaction within P3. Representation judged solely by downstream reasoning.

Each rejection is documented to preserve the rationale behind the final protocol.

## 8. Methodological Contributions

Current candidates: Atomic Content Mapping; Representation Taxonomy; Content Mapping Matrix; Representation Certification; Assumption Back-Propagation; Canonical Assumption Closure; Tiered Computational Equivalence; Representation Audit Framework; Diagrammatic Neutralization; Controlled Visual Notation Specification; Representation-independent Ground Truth; Hierarchical Confirmatory Decision Tree.

These may ultimately warrant publication as methodological contributions irrespective of the empirical outcomes of P3.

## 9. Deferred Research

Reserved for future investigation — EXP-P4: interaction between structural invariants. Candidate topics: invariant interaction; interference; resonance; attractor behaviour; field dynamics; representational evolution; hybrid invariant systems; neo-hieroglyphic representations. None forms part of P3.

## 10. Lessons Learned

Representation is scientifically independent of information. Accessibility is part of computational equivalence. Methodological rigor requires explicit certification rather than assumed equivalence. Protocol engineering begins only after conceptual stability has been achieved. Reviewer interaction naturally evolves from architectural critique toward instrumentation and execution. Separating representation from structural knowledge significantly clarified the overall Constitutional Cybernetics research programme.

## 11. Research Programme Inflection Points

P1 — Reasoning compression. Fundamental insight: structural invariants function as compressed reasoning rather than compressed information.
P2 — Consequence. Fundamental insight: structural validity and consequential validity are experimentally separable.
P3 — Representation. Fundamental insight: representation is experimentally separable from information.
Future — Interaction. Hypothesis reserved: structural invariants may themselves possess interaction dynamics independent of any particular representation.

## 12. Readiness Assessment

Following multiple internal revisions and two substantive adversarial review cycles, EXP-P3 has reached architectural stability.

The experiment now possesses: a clearly defined scientific question; explicit separation from P1 and P2; a controlled representation taxonomy; audited informational equivalence procedures; a representation certification framework; tiered computational claims; preregistered inferential architecture; controlled visual notation; explicit claims discipline.

Remaining work concerns: protocol consolidation; statistical parameterization; implementation; pilot execution; preregistration. No further conceptual redesign is currently anticipated unless new evidence reveals a fundamental flaw.

## Closing Reflection

EXP-P3 marked a transition in the Constitutional Cybernetics programme from studying what structural invariants are to studying how they are represented. In doing so, it reframed representation from a passive vehicle for knowledge into an active computational variable worthy of scientific investigation. Regardless of the empirical outcome, the experiment has already produced a set of methodological advances that are expected to inform subsequent research across the programme. The deliberate deferral of interaction hypotheses to a future P4 preserves the conceptual integrity of P3 while establishing a coherent trajectory for the next phase of the research programme.

# PART D — Claude Code Implementation Brief (GOVERNING INSTRUCTION)

## Objective

The EXP-P3 protocol has reached conceptual maturity through multiple internal design iterations and adversarial review cycles. The objective of this task is not to redesign the experiment. The objective is to transform the existing consolidated protocol into a publication-quality documentation set that separates constitutional specification, executable protocol, reusable methodology, statistical analysis, implementation details, and research provenance. This is primarily an editorial and architectural refactoring exercise. The scientific content should remain materially unchanged.

## Primary Goals

Restructure the existing consolidated document into a coherent documentation hierarchy. Reduce duplication. Improve navigability. Separate reusable methodology from experiment-specific content. Create a stable documentation architecture suitable for: scientific review; preregistration; publication; long-term maintenance.

## Critical Constraints

DO NOT: redesign the experiment; introduce new hypotheses; change scientific conclusions; alter claims discipline; rewrite the Decision Procedure; materially modify BP; materially modify VN; materially modify Tier Architecture. These sections have already undergone multiple review cycles and should be preserved wherever possible.

DO: reorganize; consolidate; remove duplicated explanations; improve internal references; improve document hierarchy; standardize terminology; improve readability.

The result should resemble a mature scientific standards package rather than a single monolithic experiment document: EXP-P3 becomes the scientific protocol; RSS-001 becomes the reusable methodology standard; SAP becomes the statistical contract; Implementation becomes the laboratory manual; Research Record preserves provenance.

The refactoring is successful if: no scientific content is materially altered; document responsibilities are clearly separated; duplication is substantially reduced; navigation is improved; terminology is consistent throughout; reusable methodology is extracted into RSS-001; EXP-P3 reads as a concise constitutional protocol suitable for external scientific review.

## Final Note

This is not a content-generation task. Treat it as a documentation architecture and standards refactoring exercise. Preserve the scientific intent and wording wherever possible, and focus on reorganizing the material into a coherent, maintainable documentation set. The objective is to make the documentation easier to review, reference, extend, and eventually publish, without changing the underlying science.
