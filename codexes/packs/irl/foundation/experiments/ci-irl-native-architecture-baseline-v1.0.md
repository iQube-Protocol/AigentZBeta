# Part I — Constitutional Internet (CI)

## 1. Native purpose

The Constitutional Internet is the constitutional architecture for preserving personhood, rights, authority, delegation, privacy/confidentiality, standing and legitimate participation across digital systems and organizational boundaries without requiring identity to become the universal root of authority.

Its native problem is not model reasoning or organizational topology. It establishes the constitutional conditions under which persons and their delegates can participate in open digital society while preserving sovereign personhood, proportional disclosure, bounded agency and evidence of consequence.

Native compression:

> **Personhood establishes the constitutional subject; identity provides contextual recognizability; delegation conveys bounded authority; constitutional evidence preserves accountability without requiring indiscriminate exposure.**

## 2. Owned primitives / responsibilities

### 2.1 Sovereign personhood / originating subject
CI treats personhood as constitutionally prior to identity. Human-originated authority ultimately traces to a continuous person rather than to a login, enterprise directory entry, wallet key or contextual identifier.

### 2.2 Individualization / continuity
The implementation-witnessed runtime distinguishes personhood, individualization, identity and standing as separate primitives. Individualization supplies continuity across time and provider changes without requiring persistent identity exposure.

### 2.3 Contextual identity
Identity establishes contextual recognizability/representation and may vary by domain. It is not the constitutional origin of personhood or authority.

### 2.4 Polity Passport / constitutional continuity record
The Passport records constitutional continuity and participation status. Possession of a Passport does not by itself confer unilateral execution permission.

### 2.5 Bounded delegation / authority provenance
Human-originated authority may be conveyed to an agent through bounded, revocable delegation. CI owns the constitutional proposition that delegation must preserve provenance to its legitimate originating subject.

### 2.6 Constitutional confidentiality / proportional disclosure
CI owns the requirement that cross-domain proof should disclose only what the receiving context legitimately needs. Privacy is therefore not equivalent to invisibility; accountability is not equivalent to universal identity exposure.

### 2.7 Standing as constitutional participation/capability evidence
Standing is distinct from personhood and identity. It can inform how much consequential authority or trust is rationally extended without altering the underlying person's constitutional status.

### 2.8 Rights, legitimacy and constitutional participation
CI establishes the rights/legitimacy frame within which persons, agents and institutions may participate in the Polity / Constitutional Internet.

## 3. Inputs CI consumes but does not necessarily originate

- proof/evidence that a human person exists;
- contextual identity attributes or credentials when a domain requires them;
- enterprise IAM roles and organizational permissions;
- delegation intent and scope from a legitimate principal;
- contextual claims/attestations/KYC where proportionate disclosure is required;
- external legal/institutional constraints relevant to rights and legitimacy;
- evidence of actions and consequences produced by runtime systems;
- standing/evidentiary updates derived from observed behavior.

CI may constitutionalize these inputs without claiming to originate all of them.

## 4. Outputs / interfaces CI can expose

- a continuous constitutional subject / personhood continuity reference;
- privacy-preserving subject commitments suitable for cross-domain proof;
- contextual identity projections where needed;
- Passport / participation state;
- bounded delegation / authority provenance evidence;
- disclosure decisions and proportionate credential presentation;
- constitutional rights/constraint context;
- standing / participation evidence;
- the constitutional basis on which another system may assess whether a claimed authority is legitimate.

## 5. Native assumptions

- human-originated consequential authority ultimately requires a legitimate originating subject or lawful institutional source;
- identity is contextual and need not be globally interoperable for authority provenance to be verifiable;
- open agentic participation creates stronger privacy/confidentiality requirements than a single closed trust domain;
- delegation is bounded and revocable rather than a permanent transfer of sovereignty;
- evidence and accountability can be preserved without exposing all identity/context information;
- existing IAM can remain useful and need not be replaced by CI.

## 6. Explicit non-responsibilities

CI does **not**, merely by establishing personhood/rights/authority provenance:

- discover or validate computational invariants;
- determine the minimum sufficient invariant substrate for a reasoning task;
- forecast the consequences of a proposed action;
- determine present runtime state;
- guarantee execution of a permitted action;
- own an enterprise's IAM implementation or organizational hierarchy;
- own OCSGA's organizational/governance topology;
- prove that a receiving system will accept a valid constitutional proof;
- make all identities interoperable.

# Part II — Invariant Research Lab / Constitutional Computing (IRL/CC)

## 7. Native purpose

The IRL is the constitutional scientific institution and experimental apparatus for determining whether invariant principles constitute measurable computational properties capable of governing intelligent/autonomous systems while preserving sovereignty, accountability, explainability, composability, predictability, observability and consequence.

Its Constitutional Computing layer is concerned with constitutional execution: policy-bound computation, workflows, bounded delegation interfaces, observability, consequence engineering and sovereignty. Its Constitutional Cybernetics layer studies feedback, adaptation, learning, multi-agent governance, resilience and optimization.

IRL-010 operationalizes the Constitutional Runtime as a set of enforced seams through which consequential activity passes rather than as a single monolithic program.

Native runtime seam, as implementation-witnessed in IRL-010:

`intent capture → context/grounding → capability evidence → consequence modeling → constitutional decision → production → constitutional validation/remediation → receipt → standing accrual → observation feeding the next cycle`

## 8. Owned primitives / responsibilities

### 8.1 Invariant
A versioned, provenance-bearing, typed computational object representing a validated knowledge claim, with lifecycle, confidence, standing, reach, contradiction, supersession and composition semantics.

### 8.2 Invariant collections / InvariantQubes / curated reasoning substrate
IRL owns the retrieval, curation and composition problem: which validated invariants should a reasoning/action process begin from, and what is the minimum sufficient constitutional substrate?

### 8.3 Invariant fields and graph relations
IRL owns typed relationships among invariants and the experimental study of fields, composition, contradiction, constraints, enablement and consequence.

### 8.4 Experimental/falsification lifecycle
IRL owns the research progression from candidate hypothesis through protocol, execution, evidence, replication and possible canonization/rejection. The Crucible is a pre-canon candidate pipeline, not a substitute for the canonical experiment registry.

### 8.5 Capability evidence
The runtime persists what capabilities exist, what is missing, what should be reused/fixed/extended and what boundaries must not be crossed.

### 8.6 Consequence modeling / consequence engineering
IRL/CC owns prospective examination of consequences and counterfactuals as computational inputs to constitutional decisions, subject to the maturity/status of the relevant implementation.

### 8.7 Constitutional decision / runtime resolution
The runtime resolves how a goal should be realized and whether constitutional constraints are satisfied before consequential production. Existing canonical runtime language uses constitutional decision; the newer Authorization terminology is separately identified below as a Crucible/canonical-amendment refinement rather than silently substituted.

### 8.8 Constitutional validation and remediation
Produced consequential artifacts/actions are evaluated against applicable constitutional constraints, with remediation/escalation/refusal paths rather than unconditional continuation.

### 8.9 Constitutional receipts / evidence
IRL/CC owns append-only evidentiary records of consequential actions/refusals and the infrastructure for provenance/anchoring of those records.

### 8.10 Standing accrual / feedback
Consequential production and observed behavior can update standing; evidence feeds later trust/governance decisions rather than terminating at execution.

### 8.11 Constitutional cybernetic feedback
The runtime treats observed outcomes as inputs to future behavior, research and governance. Consequence is therefore not merely an audit artifact but part of a learning/adaptation loop.

## 9. Inputs IRL/CC consumes but does not necessarily originate

- human/operator intent;
- current problem/task context;
- authority/delegation/personhood evidence from CI or other legitimate sources;
- contextual identity/IAM/organizational role evidence where required;
- proposed actions/plans;
- capability inventory and external system affordances;
- current environmental/operational state;
- applicable constitutional/policy invariants;
- observed execution/output and consequence evidence;
- external constraints, including safety/legal/organizational rules;
- model/tool outputs used as apparatus rather than treated as constitutional authority.

## 10. Outputs / interfaces IRL/CC can expose

- curated invariant slices / constitutional context;
- capability evidence and gap analysis;
- consequence projections / counterfactual forecasts where implemented;
- constitutional decision/resolution outcomes;
- validation/remediation/refusal results;
- receipts and provenance evidence;
- standing updates;
- experimental findings, contradiction evidence and invariant lifecycle changes;
- state/feedback evidence suitable for subsequent cycles;
- machine-readable decision/evidence objects that downstream execution systems may consume.

## 11. Native assumptions

- constitutional principles can be represented as first-class computational objects and empirically tested for computational effects;
- structural and constitutional invariants are categorically distinct: structural invariants are descriptive hypotheses; constitutional invariants are normative design choices whose computational effects can nevertheless be tested;
- consequential activity should pass through enforced constitutional seams rather than parallel bypass paths;
- evidence, contradiction and supersession must be first-class so the architecture can falsify itself;
- the platform is research apparatus; experiments should run against the same substrate used by consequential runtime systems where possible;
- consequence and observation matter to future governance/standing, not only retrospective audit;
- computation does not itself originate legitimate human authority.

## 12. Explicit non-responsibilities

IRL/CC does **not**, merely by computation or invariant resolution:

- create sovereign human personhood;
- manufacture legitimate human authority without a legitimate source;
- own all contextual identity or enterprise IAM state;
- define another independent system's organizational governance architecture;
- guarantee that an external actuator/executor actually performs an authorized/approved action;
- make consequence forecasts equivalent to realized consequence;
- treat model output, popularity or usage as sufficient epistemic/constitutional standing;
- canonize candidate claims merely because they are useful or elegant.

# Part III — Native CI ↔ IRL/CC relationship

## 13. The seam between constitutional subject and constitutional computation

The strongest native interface visible before any OCSGA comparison is:

`CI: subject / rights / authority provenance / delegation / confidentiality`

→ consumed by

`IRL/CC: invariant-grounded constitutional computation / consequence engineering / decision / evidence / feedback`

CI answers primarily **who/what the constitutional subject is, where legitimate human-originated authority comes from, and how that authority can travel without unnecessary identity exposure**.

IRL/CC answers primarily **how consequential computation is grounded, constrained, evaluated, evidenced and updated**.

This is a native CI↔IRL compositional hypothesis, not an OCSGA claim.

## 14. Shared objects with split responsibility

| Object | CI native responsibility | IRL/CC native responsibility | Status before OCSGA comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personhood | originating subject / continuity basis | consumes personhood evidence; cannot originate it computationally | clear internal seam |
| Identity | contextual representation / proportional disclosure | consumes when relevant to context; protects identifier tiers in runtime | clear internal seam |
| IAM role | external organizational authority input; constitutionalized rather than replaced | consumes role/policy evidence where required | external input to both; ownership remains enterprise/domain-specific |
| Delegation | bounded conveyance / authority provenance | evaluates/uses delegation within consequential runtime | clear internal seam |
| Authority | legitimate provenance and bounds | consumes/verifies as runtime input; may gate consequential action | clear internal seam, with newer Authority/Authorization refinement below |
| Invariants | constitutional/normative principles may originate in CI doctrine | computational representation, lifecycle, retrieval, composition, experiment | IRL computational ownership; CI doctrinal source where applicable |
| Consequence | constitutional significance/accountability | prospective modeling + retrospective evidence/feedback | IRL/CC operational ownership; shared constitutional significance |
| Evidence | constitutional requirement for accountability/confidentiality | receipts, provenance, validation, anchoring, experimental evidence | implementation primarily IRL/CC |
| Standing | constitutional participation/capability concept | computational accrual/evidence/trust-band mechanisms | shared concept, runtime implementation IRL/CC |

## 15. Explicit CI/IRL non-merger rule

The two systems should not be collapsed into one merely because they are mutually reinforcing.

- Personhood/authority provenance is not reducible to invariant computation.
- Invariant/consequence computation is not reducible to identity/delegation infrastructure.
- Constitutional doctrine and scientific validation are related but not identical activities.
- The runtime may implement CI doctrine without thereby owning the source of the doctrine.

# Part IV — Candidate extensions captured in the Crucible

The following are **not silently promoted into the native canonical baseline**. They are recorded because they materially affect the boundary comparison and are already in the Crucible / recent canonical amendments.

## 16. Authority versus Authorization

Current distinction:

- **Authority** — a governance primitive: legitimate provenance and durable bounds under which an actor may act.
- **Authorization** — a state primitive: whether this proposed action may execute now.

Temporal distinction:

- Authority is comparatively durable.
- Authorization may resolve at transaction/machine pace.
- Governance need not be rewritten merely because runtime state changes.

Candidate/runtime compression:

`Governance → Authority + Mandate + Control conditions`

`Proposed Action + Current State + applicable bounds → Authorization`

## 17. Consequence projection versus realization

Candidate distinction:

- **Current State** — what is true now.
- **Projected Consequence** — anticipated state transition if the proposed action executes.
- **Authorization** — whether that projected transition may proceed under current bounds/state.
- **Execution** — whether the action actually occurred.
- **Realized Consequence** — what state transition actually occurred.
- **Projection–realization delta** — evidence about calibration, hidden state, error and risk-of-repair.

Candidate loop:

`State₀ → Consequence Projection → Authorization → Execution → Realized Consequence → Evidence → State₁`

## 18. Trusted Intelligence / Trusted Automation

Crucible candidate vocabulary:

- **Trusted Intelligence** — consequence-aware, constitutionally governed human–synthetic intelligence whose combined capability can be rationally relied upon because Authority, Legitimacy and Accountability establish Trust and consequential evidence continuously tests/updates it.
- **Trusted Automation** — governed translation of Trusted Intelligence into consequential action, with constitutional bounds, state/consequence assessment, Authorization before Execution, and evidence of realized consequence.

These terms are useful test objects for boundary comparison but are not assigned to OCSGA and must not be used to reinterpret OCSGA's native vocabulary before exchange.

# Part VI — Pre-exchange comparison contract

## 19. Questions to apply once OCSGA baseline arrives

For every seam/object:

1. Do both systems define the object?
2. If yes, are the semantics actually equivalent or merely linguistically similar?
3. Who originates it?
4. Who owns authoritative state/custody?
5. Who verifies it?
6. Who transforms/resolves it?
7. Who consumes it?
8. What evidence crosses the interface?
9. What remains confidential?
10. What happens on revocation, stale state, disagreement, failure or contradictory authority?
11. Can the systems compose without material redefinition?
12. If not, would a new interface be useful — and would that constitute deliberate co-design?

## 20. Required seam classification

Each proposed CI/IRL ↔ OCSGA seam must be classified as:

- **COMPATIBLE**
- **AMBIGUOUS**
- **CONFLICTING**
- **REDUNDANT**
- **UNRESOLVED**

Additionally record:

- **DISCOVERED COMPATIBILITY** where native interfaces compose without material alteration;
- **CREATED COMPATIBILITY** where useful composition emerges only after redesign, reinterpretation, extension or a new co-designed interface.
