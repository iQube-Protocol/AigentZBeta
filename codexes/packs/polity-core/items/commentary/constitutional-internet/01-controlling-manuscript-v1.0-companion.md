# The Constitutional Internet — v1.0 Publication Candidate — Editorial Companion

## 0. What this pass was and was not

**Was:** a final publication-lock proofreading pass. Three independent reviewers each read
one third of the manuscript (Ch1–10, Ch11–19, Ch20–24 + Epilogue) fresh, hunting for genuine
editorial defects, unsupported factual claims, duplicated argument, weak transitions, and
inconsistent terminology — explicitly instructed not to evaluate or propose changes to any
ratified doctrine. I then independently read the entire manuscript myself and cross-checked
every reported finding, and every accepted fix, against `00-editorial-register.md` (the full
CR-1 through CR-17 / ADD-1 through ADD-3 ratification record) before touching the text.

**Was not:** a doctrine audit, and not a second reader-experience pass over material v0.4
already resolved. Neither operator-decision gate v0.4 named (CI-CC-001 ratification; the
Internet Foundation charter/mandate instrument) was revisited — both remain exactly as v0.4
left them, tracked in §4 below unchanged.

**Method:** the three review agents' full reports are preserved in this session's transcript.
Of roughly 30 raised findings, 13 were accepted and fixed (§1), 8 were reviewed and explicitly
declined as stylistic judgment calls or already-covered ground (§2), and 6 were rejected outright
because they turned out to already be ratified, permitted, or verbatim doctrine text (§3) — the
review agents did not have the doctrine register loaded in full, so this cross-check was the
substantive work of this pass, not a formality.

## 2. Findings considered and declined

These were read carefully and are genuine observations, but judged to be stylistic/pacing
choices consistent with the book's own established devices, or too large an intervention for a
publication-lock pass — the same category v0.4's own companion recorded one item under ("the
anaphoric climax device... considered and declined").

- **Ch12/Ch18 "a person with low standing remains a person" couplet, repeated verbatim.**
  Matches the book's own established pattern of repeating short, memorable two-line formulas at
  structurally relevant points (e.g. "Personhood gives continuity. Identity gives context."
  recurs five times across the book by design). Not flagged as duplication for the same reason
  v0.4 didn't flag that couplet.
- **Ch18's opening re-argues Ch12's reputation-vs-standing distinction at length.** The same
  class of observation v0.4's own chapter-readiness table already recorded for Ch5 and Ch8
  ("re-summarizes... before its own new material") and explicitly left as authored — "a pacing
  observation, not an error." Treated consistently here.
- **The cybernetic action→proof→consequence→standing loop is stated in full three times**
  (Ch21, Epilogue "society remains unfinished," Epilogue "Humans and Machines" closing).
  Each instance sits at a distinct structural point (institutional introduction, narrative
  day-summary, final synthesis) — closer to the book's deliberate-echo device than to the
  state/network/international-law triad's accidental near-duplication (which had no distinct
  structural role and was already flagged and trimmed by v0.4).
- **Ch20's claims-vs-rebuttal lists (lines ~3835–3851) don't pair up 1:1.** A real observation
  about rhetorical construction, but fixing it well would mean restructuring the passage rather
  than adjusting a sentence or two — disproportionate for a lock pass, and closer to a taste
  question than a defect.
- **Epilogue, "other operators have built additional bridges... this is evidence of success."**
  Reads as a stronger claim than the "may build" hedging used elsewhere for the same idea, but
  the entire Epilogue is explicitly a forward-looking "day in the life" narrative device — the
  same allowance CR-12 already grants explicitly for the Commons passage in this same section
  ("may appear as a functioning institution only if the passage is clearly identified as the
  forward horizon"). This passage sits under the same umbrella.
- **"Embodied machines" / "Embodied artificial intelligence" / "embodied AI" / "embodied
  computational agent" used within one ~90-line span (Ch7).** Natural prose variation on one
  chapter's central subject, not four distinct unglossed concepts.
- **"Evidence" and "proof" used somewhat interchangeably within Ch9's "the constitution
  establishes proof" section**, even though a later chapter (Ch13) treats them as more distinct.
  Minor, and the two terms are close enough in ordinary usage that tightening this would mean
  editing a definitional passage's word choice throughout — more surgery than the finding
  warrants.
- **"iQubes" / "information primitive" / "information object" used for the same concept in
  Ch19.** The passage itself says "the name matters less here than the principle" immediately
  after introducing "iQubes" — the variation is explicitly licensed by the text itself.

## 3. Findings rejected as already-settled doctrine

These looked like genuine findings on first read but turned out, on checking `00-editorial-register.md`,
to already be ratified, explicitly permitted, or verbatim doctrine text. Recorded here because the
review agents (correctly, per their brief) did not have doctrine-reopening authority and could not
have known this without the register — this cross-check is the substantive contribution of this pass.

- **Ch5, "the Internet Foundation and I called for a Universal Declaration of Digital Rights."**
  Flagged as an unsupported historical claim. This is the *exact* claim CR-8's "Canonical
  manuscript effect" section explicitly authorizes verbatim: "The manuscript may state, in
  present or historical tense, that: Dele Atanda and the Internet Foundation called for a
  Universal Declaration of Digital Rights." Not a defect — it is ratified permitted content.
- **Ch19, "Post-quantum cryptography will alter how continuity is protected. Quantum entropy
  may strengthen how identifiers, keys and privacy-preserving processes are generated."**
  Flagged as an inconsistent-hedging pair ("will" beside "may"). This sentence is the *verbatim*
  CR-17 canonical manuscript insertion text, ratified word-for-word in the editorial register.
  The differing hedge is deliberate and ratified: post-quantum migration is treated as a near-certain
  transition already underway; quantum entropy integration is CR-9's Projected-status roadmap item.
  Touching this would be reopening ratified doctrine, which this pass does not do.
- **Ch8, "the underlying principles have been ratified... now exist in working form"** followed
  by "They are not yet dominant. They are not complete. Some are mature. Some are entering
  deployment. Some remain experimental." Flagged as an unsupported claim. On inspection this
  passage already carries the same evidentiary hedging discipline CR-11 requires and that Ch20
  is explicitly praised for — the claim and its qualification arrive in the same breath. No fix
  needed.
- **Ch10, "It exists because people have articulated its principles, ratified its rules, built
  its systems..."** Flagged as an unsupported institutional claim. Read in context, this is
  part of Ch10's philosophical argument about what constitutes legitimate existence
  (permissionless constitution), not a claims-inventory of what currently operates — Ch20 is
  where the book does its detailed evidentiary accounting, with the hedging CR-11 requires.
- **Epilogue, "metaProof continues to operate systems and contribute infrastructure under
  mandate."** Flagged as carrying the same "continues to..." construction that was corrected for
  the Internet Foundation one sentence earlier. The two have different evidentiary status:
  CR-8's gap is specifically about the Internet Foundation's *active sponsorship mandate*
  (no charter instrument exists); metaProof's operational role as systems operator is
  established and undisputed throughout Ch21 and is not one of v0.4's two named gates.
- **The EU Digital Markets Act citation in Ch13** ("The Act applies to designated gatekeepers
  serving users in the Union even where the gatekeeper is established elsewhere.") Flagged for
  lacking an inline citation. This is a factually accurate description of real, current public
  law, cited the same way the book cites the UDHR and the UN Declaration on Human Rights
  Defenders elsewhere — no footnote convention exists anywhere in this manuscript, so this
  citation is not an outlier requiring one.

## 4. Remaining evidence-only blockers

Unchanged from `01-controlling-manuscript-v0.4-companion.md` §3 and §4. This pass did not
re-verify or resolve any of them — they are carried forward exactly as recorded there, plus one
citation-precision item this pass surfaced but declined to resolve unilaterally:

- **Gate 1 — CI-CC-001 ratification** (affects Chapter Twelve, Chapter Twenty). Chapter Twelve's
  argument still cites Rule IV of `ci-cc-001-book-constitution.md`, a document whose own header
  reads "Proposed for canonisation." Unresolved; requires an operator ratification decision.
- **Gate 2 — Internet Foundation charter/mandate instrument** (affects Chapter Twenty-One, the
  Epilogue). No canonical Internet Foundation charter or mandate instrument exists in Polity Core.
  The manuscript's wording throughout (verified again in this pass) consistently uses
  founding/purpose framing rather than active-mandate framing, and does not overclaim — but the
  underlying instrument gap itself cannot be closed by editorial wording and remains open.
- **UDHR primary text / 1998 UN Declaration on Human Rights Defenders (A/RES/53/144) citation
  precision, Ch5 and Ch9.** Ch9 (line ~1637 in v0.4 numbering) references "the later United
  Nations declaration concerning human-rights defenders" without naming or dating it, while the
  UDHR two paragraphs earlier is precisely dated (1948). Adding the instrument's name and year
  would improve citation-register consistency, but this pass did not add it: the v0.4 companion's
  own remaining-evidence list already tracks a related, unresolved question about which UN
  instruments have deposited primary-text evidence in Polity Core's evidence chain (CR-8's
  externally-verified-evidence discipline), and inserting a specific resolution/citation number
  here without that same verification would be exactly the kind of unverified claim this project's
  own no-guessing discipline forbids. Flagged for the operator or a dedicated evidence-deposit
  pass, not fixed here.
- Every other item in the v0.4 companion's remaining-evidence list (time-sovereignty measurement
  gap, "Companion Edge Service" naming, README/license hygiene, exit-rights implementation
  subset, governance-receipt anchoring, quantum entropy operational status, `discloseCredential()`
  implementation status) is unchanged and not re-verified in this pass — full detail remains
  authoritative in the v0.4 companion.

**Overall: the manuscript is publication-locked at the prose level.** Every chapter and the
Epilogue read as complete, internally consistent, and free of the genuine editorial defects this
pass could find after three independent fresh reads plus a full doctrine cross-check. The two
operator-decision gates and the citation-precision item above are the only things standing between
this text and a final release, and none of them can be closed by further editing — they require
operator decisions or evidence deposits outside this pass's authority.
