## ⚠️ Contradicted or unsupported by Volume I — ruling required

### V-1 · The **Internet Foundation does not appear in Volume I at all**
`grep -c "Internet Foundation"` over the deposited source returns **0**.

Volume II Ch5 states: *"the Internet Foundation and I called for a Universal Declaration of Digital
Rights."* Volume I attributes the call **to the author and to NEO-citizens**, never to an institution:

> *"I believe **NEO-citizens will call for** a Universal Bill of Digital Rights anchored to the
> Universal Bill of Human Rights…"* (Ch2)

**Consequence.** Depositing Volume I closes the *twenty-one-principles* half of G-2 but **does not**
corroborate the Internet Foundation's role. This compounds discrepancy **A-4** (the Internet
Foundation has no instrument anywhere in the repo). The attribution still requires dated Internet
Foundation archival material.

### V-2 · **"Bill" vs "Declaration"** — terminology drift
Volume I consistently says **"Universal Bill of Digital Rights"** (title of Appendix 1, and every
in-text reference). Volume II Ch5 says **"Universal Declaration of Digital Rights."**

**Ruling needed:** which is canonical? Recommend adopting Volume I's **"Bill"** for the historical
claim (it is what the source says) and noting any later renaming explicitly, rather than
retrofitting "Declaration" onto a 2009/2013 source that does not use it.

### V-3 · Volume I's own internal inconsistency: **"Universal Bill of Human Rights"**
Volume I repeatedly writes *"the Universal Bill of Human Rights"*. The actual UN instrument is the
**Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR, 1948)** — and Volume I's **own Appendix 1 preamble
gets it right**: *"The Universal Declaration of Human Rights consists of de facto Global Citizenship
Rights…"*

**Consequence for Volume II.** Ch5 and Ch10 must cite the **UDHR** by its correct name. Do not
propagate Volume I's body-text error. If Volume II quotes Volume I on this point, quote the
Appendix, not the body.

### V-4 · **Timing of the anticipation claim**
The *"future volumes"* sentence appears in the **Preface**, which the source dates to **January
2013** (the retitled republication), not to the 2009 original. The Preface itself states that
Chapter 3's §3.1 was rewritten, the historic section moved, "Aquarian" changed to "Digitterian", and
the virtual-state section added at that point.

**Ruling needed:** if Volume II implies the *2009* text anticipated later volumes, that is
**precise-dating risk**. The safe formulation cites the 2013 edition for the anticipation and the
2009 original for the principles. Volume I does state the rest of the work *"remains the same as it
was when first published in 2009."*

## 🔓 Now partly closed: G-2 (digital-rights lineage)

**Appendix 1 — Draft Universal Bill of Digital Rights** is now an internal source: **18 articles**,
each mapped to UDHR articles (6, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18, 19, 20, 26, 27, 28). Its preamble states
the three-step lineage Volume II Ch5 argues:

> UDHR = *de facto* Global Citizenship Rights → a Bill of Digital Rights makes them *"more
> meaningful, tangible, and enforceable in the modern digital world"* → sovereign entities
> established under **popular sovereignty** monitor and uphold them.

**Directly relevant to Volume II chapters:**

- **Ch5** — the human-rights → digital-rights → personhood-rights movement now has a dated internal
  source with explicit UDHR article mappings.
- **Ch10** — the **popular sovereignty** argument for permissionless, participatory constitutional
  authority appears in Volume I (Ch2, *The Rise of the Virtual State*), citing the
  International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights Art. 1(2) and UNCLOS.
- **Ch16/Ch17** — Articles 6–13 of the draft Bill (ownership of one's identity and personal data
  sets; access and correction; control of third-party access; erasure; transparency; contextual
  constraints; notice and consent) are a **direct doctrinal ancestor** of the personhood-first
  Passport and selective-disclosure arguments.
- **Ch24** — Article 16 (freely move one's personal and identity data; asylum for identity data) is
  the **strongest internal doctrinal warrant for the exit/portability claim** flagged as
  under-implemented in discrepancy **B-2**.

**Still outstanding for the operator's external pass:** the UDHR text itself, the 1998 UN Human
Rights Defenders Declaration (A/RES/53/144), and any **dated Internet Foundation** publication of a
digital-rights declaration. Volume I supplies the *author's* draft; it does not supply the
*institutional* call or the third-party instruments.
