## Why this source matters

This article provides independent third-party evidence for the historical existence and public work
of the Internet Foundation and its Universal Declaration of Digital Rights initiative.

It supports the following canonical propositions:

1. **The Internet Foundation existed as a non-governmental organization by at least 2013.**
2. **The Internet Foundation was seeking the creation of a universal declaration of digital rights.**
3. **IAPP had covered that effort as far back as 2013.**
4. **Dele Atanda and the Internet Foundation had proposed ethical-data-use standards work to BSI before the 2017 article.**
5. **That work concerned ethical definitions and standards for privacy, ownership, consent, fair use, data transfer, correction, erasure, arbitration, and notification.**
6. **The initiative was connected to standards work beyond baseline legal compliance, including GDPR and the changing UK data-protection environment.**

## Verified source findings

The article reports that BSI Group, the United Kingdom's national standards body, was developing
standards around big data, including terms and conditions for obtaining and using data and a code of
practice for ethical data use.

It states that Dele Atanda, then identified as IBM Digital Innovation Officer, said that the
ethical-data-use proposal had been made to BSI years earlier following work supported by IBM through
the Internet Foundation.

The Internet Foundation is described as a non-governmental organization seeking the creation of a
universal declaration of digital rights.

The article further states that IAPP journalist Angelique Carson had explored the initiative as far
back as 2013.

It reports that Dele Atanda was to lead a new BSI committee examining:

- ethical definitions of privacy;
- ownership;
- consent;
- the value of data;
- fair use;
- data transfer between organizations at an individual's request;
- data correction;
- data erasure;
- arbitration;
- and notification of changes to personal data.

use through BSI.
