# The Constitutional Internet — v0.4 First Publication Candidate — Editorial Companion

## 0. What this pass was and was not

**Was:** a demanding literary read of all 24 chapters and the Epilogue, run by three independent
reviewers each covering two Parts, asking one question — *does this read as a compelling book
rather than a constitutional archive?* — plus direct resolution of Chapter Twelve's missing
consequence/expulsion distinction and Chapter Twenty-One's unsupported present-tense Internet
Foundation claims, both named by the v0.3 companion as live publication gates.

**Was not:** a doctrine audit. No ratified ruling (CR-1 through CR-17) was revisited, no proposed
doctrine was ratified, and no genuinely unresolved editorial decision from the v0.3 companion's §2
was resolved by this pass — those stay open and are carried forward in §4 below.

**Method:** three parallel reviewers read Parts I–II, III–IV, and V–VI plus the Epilogue
independently, reporting exact line numbers for every finding. All edits below were made directly
against those findings; no finding was accepted or rejected without checking the actual text.

## 4. Operator-decision list

Only two items remain, both narrowed from the v0.3 companion's longer §2 by this pass's direct
resolution of the two chapters flagged as live gates. Everything else in the v0.3 companion's §2
(five-line authority formulation, plutocracy sourcing, metaMe Venture Lab ontology entry,
Commons/ontology divergence, standing glossary Appendix III, Plate count, README/license hygiene,
C-1–C-5 optional strengthening) remains exactly as recorded there — genuinely open, not manuscript
blockers, not revisited here.

### Gate 1 — CI-CC-001 ratification (affects Chapter Twelve, and Chapter Twenty via the same dependency)

Chapter Twelve's argument depends on Rule IV of `ci-cc-001-book-constitution.md` ("A right protects
legitimate human agency; it does not confer immunity from consequence") as its governing citation.
That document's own header reads "Proposed for canonisation" while its footer recommends "RATIFIED
COMMENTARY" — a document not yet ratified, cited as if it already were.

**The choice, unchanged from the v0.3 companion:** ratify CI-CC-001, or have Chapter Twelve (and
Chapter Twenty, which depends on the same document's Rule V) openly cite a proposed document as
governing. This pass's Chapter Twelve insertion ("Irrevocable citizenship is not immunity from
consequence") is consistent with Rule IV either way — it restates the rule, it does not depend on
the document's ratification status to be true. But the chapter's explicit citation practice does.

### Gate 2 — Internet Foundation charter/mandate instrument (affects Chapter Twenty-One and the Epilogue)

This pass rewrote every sentence in Chapter Twenty-One and the Epilogue that asserted the Internet
Foundation's *present-tense* institutional sponsorship as settled fact. The chapters no longer make
a claim the register doesn't support. But the underlying gap CR-8 named — "no canonical Internet
Foundation charter or mandate instrument in Polity Core defining institutional purpose, relationship
to the polity, constitutional authority and limits, relationship to metaProof and other operators,
governance and continuity, or current institutional status" — still has not been closed, and cannot
be closed by editorial rewording. It requires the operator to either supply that instrument or
confirm that none exists and the founding/historical framing this pass adopted is the permanent
resting state, not an interim one.

**Both gates share one property:** neither blocks the *prose* from being ready. Both chapters read
as complete, accurate, and consistent with everything currently ratified. What remains is a decision
about the underlying doctrine and institutional record — squarely the operator's to make, not an
editorial one.
