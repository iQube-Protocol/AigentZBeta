### ADD-1 — The Embodied Delegate (Chapter Fifteen echo + Epilogue)

**Author note (verbatim):**

> That's really good. Again, very well done. [...] One thing that I think could be interesting, we
> discussed earlier today about using TAVIS to create avatars like a digital twin. [...] there are
> scenarios where a person does want to be identifiable, with friends, with family, for example,
> [...] where they would have a delegated agent to represent them [...] an embodied version of
> themselves as an agent with bounded delegation, bounded responsibilities, bounded [...] actions.
> And that's not just transactional, it's being able to represent their point of view [...] it's
> one thing when you delegate responsibility to a third party [...] It's another thing when that
> agent is representing your likeness as well. [...] the agent isn't the person, we maintain that,
> but the agent can be a representative of the person, even down to their likeness. [...] it gives
> the whole context of bounded delegation much more gravitas and much more intimacy [...]

**Rule:** The embodied-delegate doctrine belongs in the final movement (a day in the life within
the polity) and should briefly echo back into Chapter Fifteen ("When Something Acts In Your
Name"). Governing distinctions the prose must preserve: *the agent may represent the person; it
must never become confused with the person*; and the proof question — *how can those encountering
the agent know what it is, whom it represents, what authority it possesses, and where that
authority ends?* The first-pass delivery already contains an integrated "The embodied delegate"
section in the Epilogue and a proposal/addendum copy earlier; consolidation must keep the
integrated Epilogue version and remove the duplicate proposal.

### ADD-2 — Human–Machine Constitutional Symbiosis (Epilogue)

**Author note (verbatim):**

> There was one more item which I think belongs in this book [...] our whole proposition that we're
> often forced to pit AI against humanity, and that invariance and the polity as a whole is a
> cybernetic system [...] not simply [...] a feedback loop, but [...] a codependent system [...] a
> system of symbiotic relationship where machine reasoning and human reasoning are aligned and work
> from a common substrate of invariance, structural and constitutional invariance, to progress
> society as a whole. [...] it belongs in the epilogue section after we've described what a day
> [...] a day in a life in the polity. It's not in the life of the polity. The person is the
> subject. The polity is the context.

**Rule:** Place the "Humans and Machines in Constitutional Society" section in the Epilogue, **after**
the lived-day sequence and **before** the final reader invitation. The epilogue progression must be:
*a person's day in the polity → the wider human–machine relationship → the reader's material
invitation to cross → the opening into Volume III*. Keep the emphasis: **the person is the subject;
the polity is the (constitutional) context.** Resolve the AI-versus-humanity framing rather than
reopen it. The first-pass delivery already contains the integrated section in the Epilogue plus a
proposal copy earlier; consolidation keeps the integrated version and removes the duplicate.
