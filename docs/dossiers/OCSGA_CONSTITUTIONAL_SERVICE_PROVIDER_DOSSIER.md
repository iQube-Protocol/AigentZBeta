## 1. Origin and why this dossier exists

Ian McCoy describes OCSGA — the **Operational Control System for Governed Autonomy** — around the operational question:

> **Should this autonomous decision be allowed to execute?**

That question appears strongly complementary to the Constitutional Internet and Invariants Research Lab (IRL) architecture.

The emerging separation is:

- **Constitutional Internet:** *By what authority may an intelligent system act?*
- **IRL:** *What invariant conditions must remain true as intelligence and autonomy scale?*
- **OCSGA:** *Should this specific autonomous decision be allowed to execute?*

The common premise is:

> **Capability does not create legitimacy.**

A particularly important observation from Ian in the origin discussion is:

> **“The distinction between capability, authority, delegation, and control may ultimately prove more important than the distinction between human intelligence and artificial intelligence.”**

Ian further identified mandate expansion as a central governance problem: increasingly capable systems must not gradually expand or redefine their own mandate merely because their capabilities increase.

Ian's subsequent response to the candidate invariant was deliberately cautious and constructive: he agreed that **“Authority is invariant with respect to capability”** appears to get to the heart of the problem, while explicitly wanting to understand the boundaries between CI, IRL and OCSGA before assuming alignment. That boundary-testing posture should govern the relationship-development process.

## 2. Constitutional thesis

The potential relationship is deeper than conceptual alignment.

A **Constitutional Service Provider** should be able to operate as a specialist capability inside a constitutional envelope without becoming the source of that envelope.

For OCSGA, the prospective chain is:

**Person → Authority → Mandate → Delegation → Proposed Act → Consequence → Execution Decision → Evidence / Receipt**

The Constitutional Internet establishes and carries legitimate authority and delegation. A governed-autonomy service such as OCSGA could evaluate the operational execution boundary. OCSGA need not become the source of the originating mandate, and the Constitutional Internet need not reproduce OCSGA's specialist operational-governance capability.

This is the architectural seam the relationship should investigate.

## 3. Candidate invariants surfaced by the discussion

### 3.1 Authority is invariant with respect to capability

> **An increase in an agent's capability, intelligence, autonomy or effectiveness shall not, by itself, increase the scope of its authority.**

In shorthand:

**Capability ≠ Authority**

**Delegation ≤ Authority**

**Execution ≤ Delegation**

This is a candidate IRL invariant and requires research and validation before canonisation.

### 3.2 The invariants must not be delegated away with the work

Research and operational processes may become progressively automated without surrendering the invariant conditions that make their execution legitimate, governable and trustworthy.

Automation therefore raises a prior question:

> **What must remain invariant before this task may be delegated?**

### 3.3 Bounded execution

A working formulation emerging from the discussion is:

> **Bounded execution is permission to act within a delegated mandate, conditional upon the anticipated consequences remaining within constitutionally acceptable bounds.**

This extends bounded delegation into the execution domain. It is a research hypothesis, not yet a canonical definition.

### 3.4 Context propagation does not imply authority propagation

Multi-agent systems create an important candidate invariant:

> **Delegation does not propagate merely because context propagates.**

An agent receiving another agent's context, instructions or history does not thereby inherit the originating agent's authority. This distinction should be tested against OCSGA's treatment of multi-agent and swarm execution.

## 4. Why consequence is central

Consequence is the bridge between authority and execution.

- **Capability** asks what can be done.
- **Authority** asks from whom the right to act originates.
- **Mandate** specifies what has actually been authorised.
- **Delegation** specifies what another actor or agent may do under that mandate.
- **Consequence** evaluates what happens if the proposed action enters the world.
- **Control** determines the conditions under which execution proceeds, is constrained, escalated or refused.
- **Evidence / receipt** records what was authorised, decided, executed and observed.

Three consequence surfaces are relevant:

1. **Prospective consequence** — anticipated effects, affected parties, reversibility, uncertainty, externalities and worst credible outcomes before execution.
2. **Observed consequence** — comparison of actual outcomes with anticipated outcomes after execution.
3. **Learned consequence** — evidence from outcomes feeds back into future delegation and execution bounds.

This produces a possible constitutional control loop:

**Mandate → assess consequence → bound delegation → execute → observe consequence → receipt → learn → revise bounds**

## 5. Context Erosion as a structural invariance problem

Ian's **Context Erosion** work should not be interpreted only through the constitutional lens. Its foundational significance for IRL may be more general: it provides a concrete domain for investigating **structural invariance under transformation**.

A system begins at state `S₀` with an operative context: objectives, constraints, definitions, assumptions, relationships, evidence, priorities, state and potentially relationships between agents. As computation proceeds through inference, summarisation, compression, memory, retrieval, delegation, agent handoff and recomposition, the representation is expected to change. The scientific question is not whether the entire state remains identical. It is which properties must remain invariant for the transformed system to retain the relevant structural integrity.

A working research formulation is:

> **Context erosion is the progressive loss of invariant properties across transformations of an information-bearing system.**

This is an IRL hypothesis inspired by Ian's work, not an attribution of his definition.

If `T` is a transformation and `I` is a relevant invariant, the requirement is not:

`S₀ = S₁`

but rather:

`I(S₀) = I(T(S₀)) = I(S₁)`

Across a long-running or multi-agent process:

`I(S₀) = I(S₁) = I(S₂) = … = I(Sₙ)`

while the states themselves may legitimately differ:

`S₀ ≠ S₁ ≠ S₂ ≠ … ≠ Sₙ`

### 5.1 Candidate structural invariant families

Structural invariants may include preservation of:

- semantic meaning;
- operative constraints;
- referential relationships;
- causal dependencies;
- provenance;
- state continuity;
- objective fidelity;
- evidentiary relationships;
- uncertainty and confidence semantics;
- role and actor boundaries;
- transformation lineage.

The list is provisional and should be investigated empirically rather than assumed.

### 5.2 Structural invariance precedes constitutional invariance

A key research proposition is:

> **Constitutional invariance depends upon structural invariance.**

A system cannot reliably preserve a mandate if it cannot preserve what the mandate means. It cannot preserve authority if provenance erodes. It cannot preserve bounded delegation if the constraints defining the boundary drift. It cannot reliably evaluate consequence if material context has structurally degraded.

This creates a possible layered taxonomy:

1. **Structural invariance** — does the relevant structure survive transformation?
2. **Epistemic invariance** — do evidence, uncertainty and knowledge relationships survive faithfully?
3. **Constitutional invariance** — do legitimate authority, mandate, delegation, consent and consequence bounds survive?
4. **Operational invariance** — does actual execution remain within those preserved conditions?

These layers are a research model to test, not a settled ontology.

### 5.3 Context Erosion as an experimental domain for IRL

Context Erosion may therefore be more than an example of why constitutional safeguards are needed. It may be an unusually useful empirical domain for investigating invariance as a fundamental property of intelligent systems.

Candidate IRL questions include:

- Which properties of context are truly invariant for a given process?
- Under which transformations must each invariant survive?
- How can preservation be measured?
- What constitutes legitimate variance versus erosion?
- Is erosion cumulative, nonlinear, reversible or contagious?
- Which transformations are especially destructive?
- Can invariants be encoded explicitly and tested at transformation boundaries?
- Can systems preserve a compact invariant structure while allowing the surrounding context to evolve, compress or change representation?
- How does structural invariant failure propagate into epistemic, constitutional and operational failure?

This moves the discussion from simply preserving large amounts of context toward preserving **the right properties of context**.

## 6. Candidate Constitutional Service: Governed Execution

**Working capability name:** `Governed Execution`

**Purpose:**

> Evaluate whether a proposed autonomous action may execute within its constitutional authority, delegated mandate, operational constraints and acceptable consequence envelope.

Candidate determinations:

- **EXECUTE**
- **EXECUTE WITH CONDITIONS**
- **ESCALATE**
- **REFUSE**

Each determination should ultimately be evidenced and receipted rather than treated as an unauditable Boolean response.

This is a capability hypothesis to test against OCSGA's actual architecture and terminology.

## 8. IRL research programme

This relationship suggests two related IRL research threads.

### 8.1 Structural Invariance and Context Erosion

Primary research question:

> **What properties must remain invariant as intelligent systems transform, compress, propagate and operationalise context over time and across agents?**

This is the more foundational scientific thread. It investigates invariance before narrowing to legitimacy or governance.

### 8.2 Constitutionally Governed Autonomous Execution

Primary research question:

> **What must remain invariant as increasingly capable intelligence progresses from recommendation to delegated decision to autonomous execution?**

This applies the invariant thesis to authority, agency, consequence and execution.

IRL's role is to discover, test and validate invariants. **AgentiQ** develops those invariants into architectures, protocols and executable intelligence. **metaMe Venture Lab** operationalises them in products, ventures and institutional environments. Operational evidence and consequence then return to research.

**Research → Develop → Operationalise → Observe Consequence → Research**

Research automation is therefore endogenous to the programme rather than a separate future concern. As high-quality research processes become more legible and invariant-preserving, progressively more work can be delegated without delegating away epistemic or constitutional control.

A useful distinction from the origin discussion is:

- **Constitutional Internet:** *By what authority may an intelligent system act?*
- **OCSGA:** *Should this specific autonomous decision be allowed to execute?*
- **IRL:** *What must remain invariant as intelligence transforms and scales, and under what epistemic conditions may autonomous research become trustworthy knowledge?*

The longer-term objective is not merely automated research, but **constitutionally governed autonomous research** built on structurally invariant research processes.
