## 1. Origin and why this dossier exists

Ian McCoy describes OCSGA — the **Operational Control System for Governed Autonomy** — around the operational question:

> **Should this autonomous decision be allowed to execute?**

That question appears strongly complementary to the Constitutional Internet and Invariants Research Lab (IRL) architecture.

The emerging separation is:

- **Constitutional Internet:** *By what authority may an intelligent system act?*
- **IRL:** *What invariant conditions must remain true as intelligence and autonomy scale?*
- **OCSGA:** *Should this specific autonomous decision be allowed to execute?*

The common premise is:

> **Capability does not create legitimacy.**

A particularly important observation from Ian in the origin discussion is:

> **“The distinction between capability, authority, delegation, and control may ultimately prove more important than the distinction between human intelligence and artificial intelligence.”**

Ian further identified mandate expansion as a central governance problem: increasingly capable systems must not gradually expand or redefine their own mandate merely because their capabilities increase.

## 2. Constitutional thesis

The potential relationship is deeper than conceptual alignment.

A **Constitutional Service Provider** should be able to operate as a specialist capability inside a constitutional envelope without becoming the source of that envelope.

For OCSGA, the prospective chain is:

**Person → Authority → Mandate → Delegation → Proposed Act → Consequence → Execution Decision → Evidence / Receipt**

The Constitutional Internet establishes and carries legitimate authority and delegation. A governed-autonomy service such as OCSGA could evaluate the operational execution boundary. OCSGA need not become the source of the originating mandate, and the Constitutional Internet need not reproduce OCSGA's specialist operational-governance capability.

This is the architectural seam the relationship should investigate.

## 3. Candidate invariants surfaced by the discussion

### 3.1 Authority is invariant with respect to capability

> **An increase in an agent's capability, intelligence, autonomy or effectiveness shall not, by itself, increase the scope of its authority.**

In shorthand:

**Capability ≠ Authority**

**Delegation ≤ Authority**

**Execution ≤ Delegation**

This is a candidate IRL invariant and requires research and validation before canonisation.

### 3.2 The invariants must not be delegated away with the work

Research and operational processes may become progressively automated without surrendering the invariant conditions that make their execution legitimate, governable and trustworthy.

Automation therefore raises a prior question:

> **What must remain invariant before this task may be delegated?**

### 3.3 Bounded execution

A working formulation emerging from the discussion is:

> **Bounded execution is permission to act within a delegated mandate, conditional upon the anticipated consequences remaining within constitutionally acceptable bounds.**

This extends bounded delegation into the execution domain. It is a research hypothesis, not yet a canonical definition.

## 4. Why consequence is central

Consequence is the bridge between authority and execution.

- **Capability** asks what can be done.
- **Authority** asks from whom the right to act originates.
- **Mandate** specifies what has actually been authorised.
- **Delegation** specifies what another actor or agent may do under that mandate.
- **Consequence** evaluates what happens if the proposed action enters the world.
- **Control** determines the conditions under which execution proceeds, is constrained, escalated or refused.
- **Evidence / receipt** records what was authorised, decided, executed and observed.

Three consequence surfaces are relevant:

1. **Prospective consequence** — anticipated effects, affected parties, reversibility, uncertainty, externalities and worst credible outcomes before execution.
2. **Observed consequence** — comparison of actual outcomes with anticipated outcomes after execution.
3. **Learned consequence** — evidence from outcomes feeds back into future delegation and execution bounds.

This produces a possible constitutional control loop:

**Mandate → assess consequence → bound delegation → execute → observe consequence → receipt → learn → revise bounds**

## 5. Candidate Constitutional Service: Governed Execution

**Working capability name:** `Governed Execution`

**Purpose:**

> Evaluate whether a proposed autonomous action may execute within its constitutional authority, delegated mandate, operational constraints and acceptable consequence envelope.

Candidate determinations:

- **EXECUTE**
- **EXECUTE WITH CONDITIONS**
- **ESCALATE**
- **REFUSE**

Each determination should ultimately be evidenced and receipted rather than treated as an unauditable Boolean response.

This is a capability hypothesis to test against OCSGA's actual architecture and terminology.

## 6. Connector hypothesis

A future metaMe / AgentiQ ↔ OCSGA connector could potentially pass a machine-readable **Constitutional Execution Envelope** containing some combination of:

- constitutional subject / originating person;
- acting agent or system;
- authority provenance;
- mandate;
- delegation bounds;
- proposed action;
- relevant operational context;
- consequence or risk envelope;
- required controls;
- evidence requirements;
- expiry and revocation state.

A governed-execution provider could return:

- execution determination;
- conditions or constraints;
- escalation requirement where applicable;
- decision evidence;
- eventual execution / consequence receipt references.

**Do not define this schema prematurely.** The next step is to compare Ian's actual OCSGA objects and architecture with the Constitutional Internet ontology and discover the natural interface rather than projecting one onto OCSGA.

## 7. IRL research programme

This relationship suggests an IRL research thread:

# Constitutionally Governed Autonomous Execution

Primary research question:

> **What must remain invariant as increasingly capable intelligence progresses from recommendation to delegated decision to autonomous execution?**

This also connects directly to research automation.

IRL's role is to discover, test and validate invariants. **AgentiQ** develops those invariants into architectures, protocols and executable intelligence. **metaMe Venture Lab** operationalises them in products, ventures and institutional environments. Operational evidence and consequence then return to research.

**Research → Develop → Operationalise → Observe Consequence → Research**

Research automation is therefore endogenous to the programme rather than a separate future concern. As high-quality research processes become more legible and invariant-preserving, progressively more work can be delegated without delegating away epistemic or constitutional control.

A useful distinction from the origin discussion is:

- **Constitutional Internet:** *By what authority may an intelligent system act?*
- **OCSGA:** *Should this autonomous decision be allowed to execute?*
- **IRL:** *Under what invariant and epistemic conditions may autonomous research become trustworthy knowledge?*

The longer-term objective is not merely automated research, but **constitutionally governed autonomous research**.

A provisional joint proposition is:

This proposition is exploratory and should be tested collaboratively with Ian.
