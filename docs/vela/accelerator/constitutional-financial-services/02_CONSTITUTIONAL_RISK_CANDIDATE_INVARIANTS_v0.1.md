# Constitutional Risk — Candidate Invariants

**Version:** 0.2  
**Date:** 9 September 2026  
**Status:** Candidate invariants; unratified  
**Relationship:** Proposed extensions to the Golden Cycle candidate invariant set (GC-I01–GC-I13).

No statement below is ratified by inclusion. Each requires adversarial review, evidence mapping and an explicit falsification or boundary rule.

## GC-I14 — Dual-Yield Observation

Invariant-guided action can create independently observable **temporal yield** and **economic yield**; market price alone does not exhaust the yield of the action.

## GC-I15 — Risk Localization

Material risk should be attributed to the smallest consequential action, process or state transition that creates, transforms or controls it.

## GC-I16 — Risk Assumption Specificity

Participation in a transaction does not by itself imply assumption of all risks arising from that transaction. Risk assumption must arise from law, contract, constitutional mandate, explicit acceptance or another evidenced authority source.

## GC-I17 — Risk Transfer Is Consequential

A material transfer of risk is itself a consequential act requiring identifiable parties, authority/mandate where applicable, explicit terms and evidence.

## GC-I18 — Risk Proportionality

The risk assigned to an actor should not exceed the consequence surface they controlled or knowingly and validly accepted, subject to applicable law and contract.

## GC-I19 — Risk Settlement Before Propagation

Where practicable, material risk introduced at a consequential transition should be resolved, accepted, transferred, mitigated, collateralized or explicitly carried before the next consequential transition.

## GC-I20 — Risk Traceability

A final economic consequence should be traceable backwards through the material risk-bearing transitions that produced or transferred it.

## GC-I21 — Minimum Disclosure for Risk

Risk evaluation, pricing or transfer should require no more disclosure than is necessary for the defined underwriting or consequence purpose.

## GC-I22 — Semi-Anonymous Execution

Where identity continuity is not required inside a confidential computation, direct identifiers should be replaced with strongly pseudonymous, transaction-scoped or otherwise minimized references while the constitutional system preserves attributable continuity outside the execution payload.

**Boundary:** "Semi-anonymous" is the metaMe lexicon for strong pseudonymization. It must not be represented as irreversible anonymization when a lawful or constitutional re-binding path exists.

## GC-I23 — Execution–Logic Separation

Proof that expected code executed faithfully on genuine trusted hardware does not prove that the code, policy, model, mandate or decision was correct.

**Consequence:** Vela attestation cannot substitute for constitutional authority, policy correctness, independent assessment or consequence validation.

## GC-I24 — Measurement Governance

Changing the trusted enclave/application measurement for a consequential service is itself a governed act because it changes which code is accepted as trusted execution.

## GC-I25 — Premium Non-Equivalence

A premium is the price offered for accepting a specified subset of risk under stated terms. It is not equivalent to total risk, expected loss, constitutional value or legitimacy.

## GC-I26 — Coverage Non-Authority

Insurance or another risk-transfer instrument does not authorize the underlying action.

## GC-I27 — Calibration Feedback

Predicted risk-of-repair, quoted premium/terms and observed consequence should remain linkable so that systematic prediction error can recalibrate models and candidate invariants.

## GC-I28 — No Hidden Risk Export

An action cannot be described as lower-risk merely because repair burden or liability is shifted outside the measured system.

**Relationship:** risk-apportionment specialization of GC-I03 (Non-exportation of repair).

## GC-I29 — Disclosure–Risk Coupling

Every material disclosure decision can alter consequence and risk distribution; therefore disclosure scope, purpose and recipient should be included in the causal record for consequential multi-party workflows.

## GC-I30 — Confidential Parameter Separation

Where proprietary rules, thresholds or pricing parameters must remain secret, they should be represented as confidential state/inputs rather than relying on the confidentiality of inspectable execution code.

## Proposed disposition

- Add GC-I14–GC-I30 to the **candidate** invariant register.
- Do not ratify through product implementation.
- Map each to operational evidence fields generated by Use Case Zero.
- Use Crystal to preserve failures, counterexamples and calibration error.
- Use Crucible/adversarial review before any promotion to canonical invariant status.


## Additional multi-party candidates — provisional local identifiers

Use MP-C01–MP-C06 until the repository register is reconciled; do not allocate new GC numbers blindly.

| Candidate | Requirement | Falsification / boundary test |
|---|---|---|
| MP-C01 Mandate-local veto | Constraints govern all affected interests; independent actions can proceed only with demonstrated separation. | B's refusal prevents A-only action despite verified independence, or A-only action consumes B's collateral. |
| MP-C02 Risk–reward correspondence | Differential entitlement follows agreed contribution or exposure actually assumed, not declared appetite. | Raise A's stated tolerance without changing deployment or obligations and A receives extra entitlement. |
| MP-C03 Economic separation | Nonparticipants do not bear unauthorized collateral, liquidity, fees, loss or repair exposure. | A separate ledger entry masks use of B's assets or an unaccepted shared dependency. |
| MP-C04 Allocation conservation | Every principal movement, gain, loss, cost and recovery reconciles under the frozen rule with deterministic rounding. | Duplicate allocation, missing minor unit, or recovery counted twice. |
| MP-C05 Collective and individual acceptability | Aggregate benefit cannot override any affected participant's mandate. | Pool passes while a participant liquidity floor fails. |
| MP-C06 Allocation binding | Participation, mandates, terms, state and action are bound to the evaluated envelope and receipt. | A changed schedule or stale state is settled using an earlier acceptable verdict. |

MP-C01 specializes GC-I16/I18; MP-C03 specializes GC-I28; MP-C06 supports GC-I20. These are unratified requirements for adversarial evaluation, not findings. Consent must be tied to exact terms; attribution of a risk origin does not establish funded assumption or legal liability.
