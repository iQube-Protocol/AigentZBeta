# Golden Cycle / Constitutional Risk — EXP-P1–P4 Alignment Extension

**Version:** 0.2  
**Date:** 9 September 2026  
**Status:** Cross-programme alignment note; does not alter frozen experiments

## 1. Existing programme remains authoritative

The current pipeline is:

**P1 Compression → P2 Consequential Performance → P3 Representation → P4 Interaction (Reserved)**

The Golden Cycle already establishes financial services as a **cross-programme research spine**. This Constitutional Risk / Vela use case is an additional bounded operational pilot within that spine.

It must not:
- alter P1's frozen primary design;
- silently add financial services to confirmatory P2A/P2B;
- collapse P2 process effects into P3 representation effects;
- promote P4 interaction/field language into a finding;
- launder product telemetry into scientific evidence.

## 2. New research layer

The new operational use case adds a high-value evidence domain around:
- multi-party information compression;
- selective disclosure;
- consequence decomposition;
- risk-of-repair;
- burden allocation;
- premium/coverage price;
- settlement;
- observed repair/claim;
- temporal and economic yield.

These should be captured in a common Golden Cycle record without changing the experiments that generated the original programme claims.

## 3. P1 — Compression

### Existing question
Can invariant representation plus runtime outperform conventional context engineering at equal token budget under controlled conditions?

### New hypothesis-generating financial surface
MoneyPenny's progressive disclosure collapse provides an operational example of **information minimization**:
- multiple party inputs;
- bounded disclosure;
- minimum-necessary execution envelope.

### Permitted use
Measure:
- original information volume;
- disclosed volume by tier;
- execution-envelope size;
- provenance coverage;
- reasoning/coordination time;
- extraction loss;
- task completion.

### Not permitted
Do not claim that Use Case Zero confirms P1. P1's registered comparator/budget design remains separate.

## 5. P3 — Representation

### Existing question
Under audited informational equivalence, does representational substrate materially affect reasoning performance?

### New financial task-regime candidate
Potential representations include:
- prose risk descriptions;
- structured `RiskSlice`;
- serialized `ConsequenceEnvelope`;
- graph representation of risk transfer;
- other audited informationally equivalent forms.

Financial tasks can provide deterministic ground truth for:
- authorization validity;
- fee calculation;
- settlement validity;
- exposure limits;
- coverage constraints;
- reconciliation.

### Not permitted
The accelerator implementation may motivate task design, but it cannot serve as P3 evidence without independent authoring, contamination control, informational-equivalence audit and preregistration.

## 6. P4 — Interaction (Reserved)

The dual Yield/Risk cycles generate candidate interaction hypotheses:
- risk-localization invariants may interact with disclosure constraints;
- authority specificity may interact with coverage/settlement;
- information minimization may reduce privacy risk while increasing uncertainty;
- temporal yield may trade off against repair exposure;
- some combinations may produce non-additive consequence.

These are **candidate interactions only**.

Use Case Zero should collect:
- invariant configuration;
- predicted value/time/risk;
- observed outcome;
- interaction notes;
- calibration error.

Only after enough evidence exists should a separate P4 protocol define:
- additive baseline;
- non-additive predictions;
- interaction thresholds;
- falsification;
- power.

## 8. Research interpretation

The accelerator pilot is valuable even without confirmatory status because it can:
- expose missing variables;
- test measurement feasibility;
- generate candidate invariants;
- estimate distributions/effect sizes;
- reveal interaction candidates;
- falsify architecture assumptions;
- provide a clean basis for future preregistration.

This preserves the research programme's anti-goalpost discipline while making Financial Services the operational proving spine.


## 9. Revised portfolio evidence programme

Portfolio allocation and underwriting now instrument the same action. Record both per-participant and collective results; never infer participant welfare from aggregate P&L.

| Programme | New hypothesis-generating surface | Required boundary |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Compression | Three private contributions collapse into one executable envelope. | Measure whether mandate, entitlement and dependency semantics survive compression; no change to frozen token-budget comparison. |
| P2 Consequential Performance | Selective participation, shared-risk refusal and repair allocation. | Compare authorized outcomes and exported burden, not raw approval rate; separate future registration. |
| P3 Representation | Equivalent prose, tables and structured participation/risk schedules. | Audit equal information; do not confuse redaction with representation effects. |
| P4 Interaction (Reserved) | Disclosure × mandate heterogeneity × process-risk coverage. | Candidate interaction only; no retrospective finding. |

Capture contribution/deployed capital, eligibility, affected interests, veto/refusal scope, risk assumed, fees/premium/recovery, liquidity retained, goal attainment and measured time-to-value. Include state/schedule/config hashes, provenance and whether outcomes are simulated, observed or counterfactual. Keep identifiable financial records in authorized private evidence stores.

Use a frozen hold comparator and, where separately designed, an indivisible shared-mandate comparator. Hold external facts, capital, horizons and valuation rules constant. Report whether a goal was never reached; do not record zero time-to-value for a refusal. Separate computation latency, authorization delay, settlement time and time to accepted benefit. Opportunity cost is counterfactual unless evidenced; tolerance alone creates no compensable loss.

Profit in a rising market is not evidence of invariant contribution. Insurance transfer does not remove system-wide exposure. Actuarial-grade calibration remains a research target requiring enough independent outcomes, uncertainty analysis and out-of-sample validation.
